## The three disjoint odd bad-prime branches

For primitive `gcd(a,b)=1`, 36-09EL proves `gcd(P,Q) in {1,2}`. Hence an odd prime cannot divide both `P` and `Q`. Since the root differences are `P^2,Q^2,D`, every odd bad prime lies in exactly one branch

```text
P-branch: q|P,
Q-branch: q|Q,
D-branch: q|D and q does not divide P*Q.
```

If `q|P`, then

```text
P=(a+b)^2-2b^2 ≡0 mod q.
```

Primitivity implies `q` does not divide `b`, so `2` is a quadratic residue mod `q`. Likewise `q|Q`, using

```text
Q=(a-b)^2-2b^2.
```

Therefore every odd P/Q-branch prime satisfies

```text
q ≡ 1 or 7 mod 8.
```

## D-branch formula

Assume `q|D`, `q∤PQ`. Modulo `q`, the two unit roots coalesce:

```text
P^2 ≡ Q^2 !=0,
y^2 ≡ X(X-P^2)^2.
```

For any local point with `X` away from the double root, the reduction identity makes `X` a square residue; if `X` lies in the `q`-adic neighborhood of `0`, both `X-P^2` and `X-Q^2` are units in the same squareclass and the original equation again forces `[X]=1`. In the neighborhood of the double unit root, `X` is itself a square unit. Hence the first Kummer coordinate is always trivial:

```text
W_q subset {1} x K_q.
```

36-09DW's local `[2]` Euler characteristic gives `dim_F2 W_q=2` at every odd prime because the curve has full rational 2-torsion. Since `{1} x K_q` already has dimension two,

```text
W_q={1} x K_q.
```

In the concatenated four-bit convention this is

```text
span{(0,0,1,0),(0,0,0,1)}.
```

This formula is independent of `q mod 8` and of the valuation of `D`.

## P-branch formula

Assume `q|P`, `q∤Q`. Reduction gives

```text
y^2 ≡ X^2(X-Q^2).
```

For unit `X`, the third factor must be a square residue and the product relation among the three full-2 Kummer coordinates gives `[X]=[X-P^2]`. Near the double root `X=0`, one has `[X-Q^2]=[-1]`, so

```text
[X-P^2]=[X]*[-1].
```

If `q≡1 mod8`, `-1` is a square and both regimes give the diagonal relation. The split norm equation near the double root realizes both squareclass directions; with the exact local dimension two this yields

```text
W_q={(c,c):c in K_q}
   =span{(1,0,1,0),(0,1,0,1)}.
```

If `q≡7 mod8`, `-1` is the nonsquare unit. The double-root equation is the norm conic `t(1-t)=square`. Here `Q_q(i)/Q_q` is unramified, so its norm squareclasses have even valuation; consequently both Kummer coordinates have zero valuation bit. Thus

```text
W_q=U_q x U_q
   =span{(0,1,0,0),(0,0,0,1)}.
```

The reverse inclusion in both cases follows from `dim_F2 W_q=2`.

## Q-branch formula

Assume `q|Q`, `q∤P`. Reduction gives

```text
y^2 ≡ X^2(X-P^2).
```

The same double-root analysis, now with the chosen second Kummer coordinate equal to `[X-P^2]`, gives:

For `q≡1 mod8`, `-1` is square and the second coordinate is trivial while the first realizes all of `K_q`:

```text
W_q=K_q x {1}
   =span{(1,0,0,0),(0,1,0,0)}.
```

For `q≡7 mod8`, the unramified norm argument again kills valuation bits and

```text
W_q=U_q x U_q
   =span{(0,1,0,0),(0,0,0,1)}.
```

Again `q≡3,5 mod8` cannot occur in the P/Q branches because `2` must be a residue.
