## 2. An original-orbit lower bound for the common denominator

Define epsilon=2 if A is even and epsilon=1 if A is odd. Section237 (237.4) and (237.5), valid for all signed n, give at every prime ell^e||D:

- odd n: v_ell(s)>=e, with one additional power at ell=2;
- even n: v_ell(r)>=2e, with one additional power at ell=2.

Because gcd(b,D)=1, these are numerator divisibilities. If A is odd, C is odd so D is odd. If A is even, the extra dyadic factor is exactly what epsilon records. Therefore

odd n: epsilon D | S0;
even n: epsilon D² | R0.                                (253.2)

The roots are positive and r,s<B. The integer numerators in (253.2) are thus at least epsilon D or epsilon D², while S0<Bb and R0<Bb. Consequently

odd n: b>epsilon D/B;
even n: b>epsilon D²/B.                                 (253.3)

This argument uses full original orbit depth and positivity. It does not infer an integer divisibility from a merely local squareclass or drop denominators. All signed parities use the same retained S237 bounds. For both parities it implies b>D/B>A, since D>1 and C>B.

## 3. Both denominator sides are compulsory

Primitive positive Pythagorean legs satisfy A>=3. The identity (C−B)(C+B)=A² and the positive integer C−B>=1 imply C+B<=A², hence C<A². In particular

sqrt(C/A)<sqrt(A)<A.                                    (253.4)

Suppose b_K=1. Then b=b_J=alpha. The strict physical alpha chamber gives b<sqrt(C/A)<A, whereas (253.3) gives b>A. Contradiction. Thus b_K>1; (253.1) gives b_J>b_K>1.

This is a uniform original-packet implication for every primitive ordered base and every signed |n|>=3. It is not restricted to H-supported denominators or finite quotient classes.

Since the two sides are positive coprime integers greater than 1, choose distinct primes ell_J|b_J and ell_K|b_K. Thus b is never a prime power. Both primes are 1 mod4 and outside 2ABC. The smallest possible b_K is 5; the smallest supported coprime b_J>b_K is at least 13. Therefore b>=65 is an optional coarse corollary, not a sufficient existence condition.

There are also quantitative lower bounds on b_K. Combining b=alpha b_K², alpha<sqrt(C/A), and (253.3), then squaring positive quantities, gives

odd n: b_K⁴>epsilon² A³ C/B²;
even n: b_K⁴>epsilon² A⁵ C³/B².                          (253.5)

The inequalities are strict. They constrain the denominator of alpha itself; this is more information than an absolute b>1. They do not bound b_K from above or empty the full receiver.

Thus the compulsory distinct ell_J and ell_K have equal v2(t_ell). Their reduction roles are Qbar=T0 and Qbar=O, respectively. S251 additionally gives, with c=1 if A even and c=2 if A odd,

## 5. Exact finite H-order collision criterion, valid for all n

For any odd ell|H, ell∤ABC and ell=1 mod8. The nodal special fiber is y²=x(x−A²)². Its nonsingular group is F_ell^× under

t=y/(x−A²), z=(t−A)/(t+A),
t_P=BC/(C−A), z_P=(t_P−A)/(t_P+A).                       (253.8)

The identity is z=1; T0 is z=-1. All denominators in the displayed formula for the finite Pbar are nonzero modulo ell. Compute t_ell=ord_Fell*(z_P) exactly by factoring ell−1 and modular exponentiation. This needs no Mordell–Weil basis, conjectural rank, saturation certificate or point search.

Define for each integer e>=0 the finite set

H_e={odd ell|H : (cA/ell)=(cC/ell)=+1,
      4|t_ell, 2t_ell|(ell−1), v2(t_ell)=e+2}.           (253.9)

The order and character tests are necessary conditions only. A prime in H_e is not a physical denominator witness without the original packet.

If the full physical denominator is H-supported, (253.6) and (253.7) put EVERY prime of b in H_{v2(|n|)}. Section3 requires a prime on each coprime side, so

H-supported full packet => |H_{v2(|n|)}|>=2.             (253.10)

In particular, if |H_e|<=1 for all e, no H-supported full packet exists at that base for ANY signed |n|>=3. Any hypothetical full packet then has an ell|b outside H; by S232 it is outside 2ABC as well, hence a GOOD-reduction denominator prime. This is an all-index good-witness theorem conditional on a computable base criterion, rather than S252's theorem only above a huge base-dependent index cap.

Consequences include every base for which H has at most one odd prime factor, and every base with at most one admissible H prime. The stronger block test also works when several primes separately pass the local tests but lie in different v2-order blocks. This does NOT exclude all good-prime packets and does NOT assert that the good-witness stratum is empty.

If a block does contain at least two primes, the H-only case remains live. For each pair ell1!=ell2 in the same H_e, let h_i=t_ell_i/4 and L=lcm(h1,h2). The simultaneous ORIGINAL order condition is exactly

n=L times an odd signed integer.                        (253.11)

Indeed h_i|n and n/h_i odd imply the rule; conversely v2(h1)=v2(h2)=e makes L/h_i odd, proving both directions. The residual H-only original indices lie in the finite union of these pair progressions. The rule is necessary for a packet and sufficient only for these two modular order equations. S252 still bounds |n| on these remaining H-supported packets. Its height estimate may use the smaller proved support H_e, as S252 already permits; no new general height theorem is claimed here.
