## Theorem 1: exact maximum for the B-boundary projection

For any positive integer F divisible by4, define
S(F)=product over primes p of p^floor(v_p(F)/2), κ(F)=F/S(F)².
κ is squarefree; it is not generally rad(F). Write a=v2(F), b=oddpart(S(F)). B²|F is equivalent to B|S(F).

The projection considered here consists exactly of positive primitive conics A²+B²=C² satisfying B²|F, with labels A,B retained.

* If a=2 or3 and b=1, this projection is empty.
* If a=2 or3 and b>=3, its exact maximum C is (b²+1)/2, attained by ((b²−1)/2,b,(b²+1)/2).
* If a>=4, its exact maximum C is S(F)²/4+1, attained by (S(F)²/4−1,S(F),S(F)²/4+1).

Proof. Put u=C−A, v=C+A; u,v positive, u<v, uv=B², C=(u+v)/2. For odd B, u,v odd and C<=(B²+1)/2, because (u−1)(v−1)>=0. If a>=4, S is divisible4 and even B<=S attains its cap with u=2,v=S²/2. A=S²/4−1 is odd and coprime to S, since every odd prime divisor of S sees A≡−1. Odd B<=b<=S/4 has C<=S²/32+1/2, strictly below the even cap. In both cases S²|F ensures the extremal conic lies in the projection. This proves emptiness, upper bound, and attainment uniformly.
