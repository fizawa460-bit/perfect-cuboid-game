## 2. All-n height proof

Since d is odd and k+N odd, squares modulo4 give F≡0 mod4: for even N, k is odd and F≡1−1; for odd N, k is even and N²−1≡0. F>0 because N>=3.

Put u=C−A>0 and v=C+A>u; then uv=B². A primitive positive integer conic has C odd and precisely one even leg, which is divisible by4 (squares modulo4 and8). If B is odd, u,v are odd positive integers, so
(u−1)(v−1)>=0 implies C=(u+v)/2<=(B²+1)/2.
Since B²|F and B is odd, B²|oddpart(F), proving (224.2). As v2(F)>=2, oddpart(F)<=F/4, so this is at most F/8+1/2, and therefore at most F/4+1.

If B is even, C,A are odd, hence u,v>=2. The inequality
(u−2)(v−2)>=0 implies C=(u+v)/2<=B²/4+1<=F/4+1.
This proves (224.1) for both parities of B and BOTH parities of n. It requires original conic positivity/primitivity; it is not an automatic height transfer to a different population.

Note the difference from a generic fixed-height estimate: the ceiling is now supplied by the SAME original scalar parameters of the physical packet. It still grows when N,d,|k| grow.

## 3. Even-n refinement and residue exclusion

For even N, k and d are odd. G cannot vanish: k=±d would contradict gcd(d,k)=1 since d>=3. Thus A²|G gives A²<=|G|, while B²|F gives B²<=F. Therefore
C²=A²+B²<=|G|+F
=d²N²+2max(0,G),
which proves the even-n height ceiling.

Odd k,d imply v2(G)>=3. Suppose v2(G)=3. A²|G forbids A even, because the even leg of a primitive conic is divisible by4. Thus A would be odd and B divisible by4. But F=G+d²N² has v2(F)=2 when N≡2 mod4, and v2(F)=3 when N≡0 mod4. Both contradict16|B² and B²|F. Hence v2(G)>=4, proving16|G, or equivalently k≡±d mod8.

If N≡2 mod4, the now-established16|G gives F≡4 mod16. B cannot be even, so B is odd and A even. These are new elementary consequences of the retained boundary conditions, not a new claim about the old odd-n A² frontier.
