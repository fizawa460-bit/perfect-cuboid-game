## 3. Uniform signed formal-unit lemma

Work on an integral model y^2=x^3+a2*x^2+a4*x+a6, a1=a3=0.
Let Z be a nonzero formal point with t=-x/y and L=v_p(t)>=1 for odd p,
or L>=2 for p=2. For every nonzero signed integer k:

t([k]Z)/(k*t(Z)) is in 1+p Z_p (odd p), or 1+8 Z_2 (p=2),
psi_k(Z)/[(-1)^(k-1)*k*t(Z)^(-(k^2-1))] is in the SAME unit group.
                                                               (265.3)
Both displayed units are p-adic squares. This is an unbounded-in-k
statement, including arbitrarily large v_p(k), not an expansion whose
error is allowed to deteriorate after division by k.

Proof. The integral local series have x=t^-2*(1+t^2 Z_p[[t^2]]) and
y=-t^-3*(1+t^2 Z_p[[t^2]]); negation is t -> -t. Multiplication series
are integral and odd. For a p-unit u the linear coefficient is u, so
t([u]Z)/(u*t) is in 1+p^(2L) Z_p. The leading term of psi_u is
(-1)^(u-1)*u*t^(-(u^2-1)); for odd u it comes from its integral x
polynomial and for even u from 2y times that polynomial. When p is odd
the factor2 is a unit; when p=2 a p-unit u is odd. All remaining powers
are at least two t-degrees deeper, giving the same conclusion.

For an odd p, the polynomial psi_p has leading coefficient p and all
other coefficients integral. After division by p its remaining terms
have valuation >=2L-1>=1. The invariant differential identity for [p]
makes coefficients of degrees2,...,p-1 divisible by p; terms of degree
>=p, after division by p*t, have valuation >=(p-1)L-1>=1. Thus both
normalized units for multiplication by p are in 1+p Z_p. For p=2,
psi_2=2y gives a unit in1+2^(2L) Z_2. Also
[2](t)=2t-2a2*t^3+O(t^5) with integral coefficients and odd powers;
after division by2t the first error has depth2L>=4 and later errors
have depth>=4L-1>=7. This is in1+8 Z_2.

Finally use psi_(ab)(Z)=psi_a([b]Z)*psi_b(Z)^(a^2), and the
multiplication-series identity, successively along k=p^r*u. Formal
depth never decreases. In the normalized composition the extra factor
is a power of t([b]Z)/(b*t), already in the same unit group. The leading
signs agree since a(a-1)(b-1) is even. Every finite composition stays
in the same unit group. Negative k follows from psi_-k=-psi_k and
t([-k]Z)=-t([k]Z). This proves both claims for ALL signed k.

- at odd p|D, t=1 mod p;

- at2, t=1 mod8 if B odd and t=-1 mod8 if B even;

The last condition is an ordinary CRT residue: write a_p=v_p(c_p),

t=p^e_p*u_p^(-1) modulo p^(e_p+1), where the unit inverse is modulo p.
