123.2 Cross-gcd canonical-square corollary


For epsilon,eta in {+1,-1}, put


  d_(epsilon,eta)=gcd(N+epsilon,K+eta).


Strong divisibility and the two canonical-base formulas imply:


if d_(epsilon,eta) is odd,


  C_d / C_1 is an integer square;


if d_(epsilon,eta) is even,


  C_d / C_2 is an integer square.


Proof.


If N and K have the same parity, the two selected neighboring denominators have the same canonical base G, so


  C_d
  =gcd(G z^2,G w^2)
  =G gcd(z,w)^2.


The parity of d matches that base: odd neighbors give C_1, even neighbors give C_2.


If N and K have opposite parity, one neighboring denominator has base C_1 and the other has base C_2=C_1 H, where


  H=2AC  in the B-even model,
  H=4AC  in the B-odd 2-minimal model.


The selected odd-index denominator satisfies


  gcd(C_j/C_1,H)=1


by the retained exact local valuation formulas:
- odd j has no A- or C-support;
- at 2, after removing C_1, the odd-index denominator has zero residual 2-valuation in both primitive parity models.


Hence the cross-gcd is


  C_1 times a square.


This is a genuine cross-index consequence, but by itself it is not a contradiction; d can be 1 or another already-normalized defect index.


123.3 Primitive-divisor transfer lemma


Let M>=3, and suppose p is a primitive divisor of C_M:


  p|C_M,
  p does not divide C_j for 1<=j<M.


The retained local formulas imply automatically


  p does not divide 2ABC.


Indeed:
- every p|B already divides C_1;
- every odd p|A or p|C already divides C_2;
- p=2 divides C_1 in the B-even model and C_2 in the B-odd 2-minimal model.


Thus for M>=3 a primitive divisor has x(R) a p-adic unit.


Primes p|D=A^2+C^2 are allowed. At such a prime the retained local analysis puts R in the nonsingular identity component E_0, so reduction of multiples remains compatible with the group law. Outside D the curve has good reduction at p.


Now take a physical reciprocal index J>=3 and set


  M=J+1.


If p is a primitive divisor of C_M, then


  (J+1)R reduces to O modulo p,


while J<M implies p does not divide C_J, so JR has finite nonsingular reduction. Therefore


  JR == -R mod p,


hence


  x(JR) == x(R) mod p,


and therefore


  v_p(H_J)>0.


For its reciprocal physical partner L, Section122 gives


  H_J H_L=x(R).


Since x(R) is a p-unit,


  v_p(H_L)<0.


But


  H_L^2=x(LR)-x(R),


and x(R) is p-integral. Negative valuation forces x(LR) to be nonintegral, hence


  p|C_L.


Strong divisibility now gives


  p | gcd(C_M,C_L)
    = C_gcd(M,L).


Primitivity of p at M forces


  gcd(M,L)=M,


so


  boxed:
  M | L.


Therefore:


PRIMITIVE-DIVISOR TRANSFER
If C_(J+1) has a primitive divisor, then


  J+1 divides the absolute reciprocal partner index.


No good-reduction-only hypothesis is being silently inserted; the retained p|D nonsingular-component case is included.

123.5 Uniform Lang inequality for this one-parameter section family


The generic E3 family is non-isotrivial and R is a non-torsion generic section. Retained results also give R_t non-torsion for every rational t>1.


Let h(t) be the absolute logarithmic height of the rational parameter.


By the standard specialization theorem for canonical heights,


  hat h_t(R_t) / h(t)
  -> hat h_generic(R) > 0


as h(t)->infinity.


Therefore there is H_0 and c_1>0 such that


  hat h_t(R_t) >= c_1 h(t)


for all rational t>1 with h(t)>=H_0.


Write t=u/v in primitive integer coordinates. The retained primitive Pythagorean integral model has


  A=u^2-v^2,
  B=2uv,
  C=u^2+v^2,


up to the harmless primitive orientation, and displayed discriminant


  Delta_disp
  =16 A^4 C^4 (A^2+C^2)^2.


If H=max(|u|,|v|), then


  log |Delta_min(E_t)|
  <= log |Delta_disp|
  <= 24 log H + O(1)
  = 24 h(t)+O(1).


Hence for sufficiently large h(t),


  hat h_t(R_t)
  >= c log |Delta_min(E_t)|


with one constant c>0 independent of t.


For bounded h(t), Northcott gives only finitely many rational t. Every retained R_t is non-torsion, so each has positive canonical height. Shrinking c over this finite set gives one uniform constant for every rational t>1.


Therefore the family of triples


  (E_t,R_t,Q_t),


where Q_t=(0,0) is rational 2-torsion, satisfies the uniform Lang-type hypothesis required by the Everest-McLaren-Ward primitive-divisor theorem.


This is an existence theorem for a uniform constant; no effective numerical c is claimed here.


123.6 Everest-McLaren-Ward uniform Zsigmondy consequence


External source lock:


G. Everest, G. McLaren, T. Ward,
"Primitive divisors of elliptic divisibility sequences",
Journal of Number Theory 118 (2006), 71-89,
Theorem 2.4.


Theorem 2.4 states, in the needed form:


For a family of globally minimal elliptic curves with a non-torsion point P and rational 2-torsion Q, a uniform Lang lower bound


  hat h(P)>=c log |Delta(E)|


gives a uniform bound for the even Zsigmondy indices. If additionally


  x(P)-x(Q)


is a rational square, the odd Zsigmondy indices are uniformly bounded as well.


For our retained section,


  Q=(0,0),


and


  x(R)-x(Q)
  =x(R)
  =(AC/B)^2


in the integral model, so the square condition is exact. Its squareclass is unchanged by passage to a global minimal model.


Combining Section123.5 with Theorem 2.4 therefore gives:


There exists an integer


  Z_B < infinity,


independent of rational t>1, such that


  C_j has a primitive divisor


for every j>Z_B.
