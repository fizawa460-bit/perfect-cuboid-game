## 1. Prime witness exists at every rational specialization

Normalize (a,b)=(A/C,B/C) to a positive primitive Pythagorean triple A²+B²=C². C is odd and gcd(A,C)=1. Put D=A²+C². Then v_2(D)=0 if A is even, and v_2(D)=1 if A is odd.

D is not a square: otherwise 1+a² would be square, contradicting the retained Section194 no-order-four/Fermat-area-one lemma (equivalently 1−a² and 1+a² square gives area one).

D is not twice a square either. Write t=P0/Q0 in positive coprime integers, P0>Q0. Direct algebra gives
1+a² = 2(P0⁴+Q0⁴)/(P0²+Q0²)².
If D=2W², then (W/C)²=(P0⁴+Q0⁴)/(P0²+Q0²)². A rational square root of the integer P0⁴+Q0⁴ is an integer, contradicting the classical Fermat theorem X⁴+Y⁴=Z² has no positive integer solution. This PLUS-sign theorem is an additional classical input, not silently inferred from the retained MINUS-sign lemma. A self-contained descent is below.

If every odd prime had even valuation in D, its v_2 value would force D=W² or D=2W². Both are impossible. Thus at least one ODD prime ell has v_ell(D) odd. Also ell does not divide AC; and ell does not divide C+A, since at a common divisor D≡2C². Consequently
v_ell(a)=0, v_ell(1+a)=0, v_ell(e_1)=v_ell(D) is odd.
Indeed ell≡1 mod4 because a²≡−1 mod ell; this congruence is descriptive, not needed for the contradiction. The witness prime may move with t: the proof does not require one fixed prime or a bounded prime list.

## 2. Complete rational local exclusion, including poles

General lemma: for an odd prime ell, a in Q_ell a unit, and v_ell(e) odd, there is no rational (or Q_ell) solution to both
1+a z²=e H² and 1−a z²=e K².

Let w=v_ell(z), allowing z=0 separately.
* z=0 or w>0: both left sides are units. Their valuations are0, whereas v(eH²),v(eK²) are odd, contradiction.
* w<0: the term a z² strictly dominates1 on both sides. Both valuations are2w, even, again impossible.
* w=0: both left sides are integral and their sum is2, a unit. At least one left side is therefore a unit, giving the same even-versus-odd contradiction. If one side is zero, the other is2 and still contradicts parity.

This exhausts all rational z and all potential denominators, cancellation and zero cases. Apply the lemma with the prime from §1 and e=e_1. Hence the entire epsilon=1 cover is empty, even without the physical interval.

## 3. Classical plus-sign descent, recorded for Drive-only continuation

Assume coprime positive x,y satisfy x⁴+y⁴=z² with z minimal. Both odd are impossible modulo16; choose x odd,y even. A primitive Pythagorean parametrization gives x²=u²−v², y²=2uv, z=u²+v², with coprime u>v of opposite parity. Reduction modulo4 makes u odd,v even. From y²=2uv obtain u=k²,v=2l². The primitive triangle x²+v²=u² has x=h²−j², v=2hj, u=h²+j² with coprime positive h,j. Since hj=l², write h=c²,j=d². Then c⁴+d⁴=u=k², another positive solution with smaller hypotenuse k<z. This contradicts minimality. Clearing denominators extends it to rational fourth-power sums.

Reference for the classical theorem: Keith Conrad, The Congruent Number Problem, Appendix B, pp.15–16, https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/ugradnumthy/congnumber.pdf. It identifies the no-positive-solution equations w²=u⁴±v⁴ and their area-one/area-two interpretation.
