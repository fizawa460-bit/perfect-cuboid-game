## Outcome

For a fixed ORIGINAL positive primitive conic A²+B²=C² and P=(AC,ABC) on E:y²=x(x−A²)(x+C²), define the nonzero INTEGER W_n=Psi_n(E,P), with the complete division-free recursion below. For ANY original H denominator prime q, put r_q=the order of the reduction of ORIGINAL P, e_q=v_q(d), M_q=q−1 and L_q=v_q(z([M_q]P)). Then

L_q=v_q(W_(r_q)), and q^(e_q+1) divides W_(r_q).       (223.1)

The second conclusion uses the retained full-M receiver L_q>=e_q+1. It is an EXACT integer necessary condition. r_q computes P's depth; it NEVER replaces the full M_q for Q. The original packet and all its masks are retained.

An explicit division-free coefficient bound now gives

0<|W_n|<=2^S_n C^(n²−1),
q^(e_q+1)<=2^S_(r_q) C^(r_q²−1).                     (223.2)

Thus the exponent in C is r_q²−1, with the terminating S_n recurrence below, instead of the unreduced weighted-chain exponent K_r from Section222. This is a uniform algebraic upper bound, NOT a true growth exponent, a canonical-height theorem, or all-height closure.

Multiple H primes on the SAME original conic can be grouped by order. For H_r={q in the packet's H support: r_q=r},

product_(q in H_r) q^(e_q+1) divides W_r,
product_(q in H_r) q^(e_q+1)<=2^S_r C^(r²−1).         (223.3)

This is distinct-prime divisibility of ONE integer, not a probability product or an independence assumption. It avoids separately charging the same archimedean budget for every prime in a group.

For ALL H primes at once, take ell=lcm(r_q) over the nonempty H support. Since ell/r_q is an integer, a strengthened joint receiver is

product_(q in H) q^(e_q+1+v_q(ell)) divides W_ell,
d_H rad(d_H) product_(q in H) q^v_q(ell) <=2^S_ell C^(ell²−1), (223.4)

where d_H=product_(q in H)q^e_q. The extra term is v_q(ell/r_q)=v_q(ell), because r_q|q−1. ell may be large; grouping by r is often the cheaper certificate. Neither bound controls the non-H part of d or empties all H packets as C grows.

## 2. Explicit division-free integer recursion

Let x=AC, y=ABC, b2=4B², b4=−2A²C², b8=−A⁴C⁴ and Y=(2y)^4. Define F_n by Psi_n=F_n for odd n and Psi_n=2y F_n for even n. Initial values:
F_0=0, F_1=F_2=1,
F_3=3x⁴+b2 x³+3b4 x²+b8,
F_4=2x⁶+b2 x⁵+5b4 x⁴+10b8 x²+b2 b8 x+b4 b8.

For odd n=2m+1>=5:
if m even, F_n=Y F_(m+2) F_m³−F_(m−1) F_(m+1)³;
if m odd,  F_n=F_(m+2) F_m³−Y F_(m−1) F_(m+1)³.
For even n=2m>=6:
F_n=F_m (F_(m+2) F_(m−1)²−F_(m−2) F_(m+1)²).

These follow by substituting the parity factors 2y in the ordinary Psi recurrences: the even recurrence's two factors of 2y cancel symbolically. There is NO arithmetic division, so the recursion works in integers, any residue ring, and polynomial rings. In particular Psi_r(P) modq^(e+1) can be computed without expanding a polynomial or constructing huge rational multiplication coordinates.

In the polynomial ring Z[A,B,C], every F_n is homogeneous of degree w_n=n²−1 for odd n and w_n=n²−4 for even n (zero F0 is handled separately). Bases have degrees0,0,8,12; Y has degree12. In each displayed recurrence the two summands have the same weight w_n by direct substitution. Induction proves the claim for every n. Restoring 2ABC for even n gives W_n homogeneous of degree n²−1. Reduction modulo A²+B²−C² is not needed for this bound.

## 5. What has and has not advanced

The single-prime and simultaneous receivers are exact necessary conditions consuming the original division mask through the retained full-M depth receiver.
