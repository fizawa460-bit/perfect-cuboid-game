## 1. Quotient-side saturation is exactly the source quotient

## 2. K is uniformly 2-saturated, including torsion translates

Known input: a is positive and not a rational square (retained Section16).
Also E has no rational point of order four. The only potentially divisible nonzero two-torsion point is (alpha,0), since the other roots have a negative root difference. Dividing (alpha,0) by two requires 1+a²=w². Combined with b²=1−a², this would give a rational right triangle of area one with legs (w−b)/a and (w+b)/a, hypotenuse 2/a. Fermat's area-one theorem excludes this. This is the familiar rational halving criterion for a curve with split two-torsion; all root differences must be squares.

Use the standard one-coordinate two-isogeny descent homomorphism delta_x:M→Q*/Q*², given by x off roots, delta_x(O)=1 and delta_x(T0)=−alpha. At the other roots delta_x(Talpha)=alpha and delta_x(Tminus)=−1. In particular delta_x(P)=[a] and delta_x(E[2]) is contained in {[1],[−1]}. (It is the Kummer map for the dual isogeny.)
Suppose 2X=jP+T with T in E[2]. Applying delta_x gives 1=[a]^j delta_x(T). An odd j would require a or −a square, impossible. Thus j=2m. Now 2(X−mP)=T. Nonzero T would give order four, impossible. Hence T=O and X−mP belongs to E[2], so X belongs to K.

Therefore M/K has no nonzero two-torsion and no nontrivial finite class of even order. This strengthens the exact adapter to all torsion translates and all ranks; it does not eliminate odd source saturation.
