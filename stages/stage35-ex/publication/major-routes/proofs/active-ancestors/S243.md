2. Universal four-factor support and parity theorem
The three square-product equations imply [F1]=[F2]=[F3]=[F4]=c in Q*/Q*². Positivity chooses c positive. Write α=m/n in lowest terms with m,n positive, and set
g1=Cm²−An², g2=Cm²+An², g3=Cn²−Am², g4=Cn²+Am².
Since n² is a square, all gi have the same squareclass c. Choose c a positive squarefree integer. If an odd prime ℓ divides c, then vℓ(gi) is odd and nonnegative for every i, hence ℓ divides every gi. The identities
g2−g1=2An², g4−g3=2Am², g2+g1=2Cm², g4+g3=2Cn²
and gcd(m,n)=1 imply ℓ|A and ℓ|C. This contradicts gcd(A,C)=1. Thus c has no odd support, so c∈{1,2}. This argument includes primes dividing A or C and denominators; it does not assume α is a unit at ℓ.

| Base parity | Forced c | Reduced α parity | Proof of dyadic selection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A even, C odd | 1 | At least one of m,n odd | If m odd then g1,g2 are odd; if n odd then g3,g4 are odd. A factor with valuation zero excludes c=2. |
| A odd, C odd | 2 | m,n both odd | Opposite parity would give a pair congruent to A,−A mod4, impossible for two odd squares when c=1. With both odd, one of g1,g2 is 2 mod4 and has valuation1. |

For the odd-A case, opposite parity makes every gi odd, so c=1 before the mod4 contradiction is used. The both-odd case uses m²≡n²≡1 mod4; one of C−A,C+A is 2 mod4. The theorem is uniform in the primitive base and α. Finite parity fixtures in the verifier support the implementation; the elementary divisibility/parity proof supplies the universal quantifier.
3. Full Kummer coset collapse
Use δ(R)=([x(R)],[x(R)−A²],[x(R)+C²]) with its usual values at 2-torsion; its kernel is 2E(Q). For Q,
δ(Q)=([D],[A c],[C c]), because J=Dα², J−A²=A F1, J+C²=C F4.
For an even A, primitive Pythagorean parametrization gives C−A=(h−k)², C+A=(h+k)². For an odd A, it gives C−A=2k², C+A=2h². Thus δ(P)=([D],[A c],[C c]) for the same forced c. Therefore Q∈P+2E(Q), uniformly for rational full-cover points. Also δ(Qsharp)=([-D],[-A c],[C c])=δ(T0)δ(P), so Qsharp∈T0+P+2E(Q).

5. Explicit half and original mod4 condition
For any retained rational physical roots with r>0, let Z=D_n−T0. Translation by T0 gives
Z=(D²/r²,−D²pq/r³).
Its three x-root differences are (D/r)², (Aq/r)², (Cp/r)². Choose ρ0=D/r, ρ1=Aq/r, ρ2=Cp/r; their product is −y(Z). The standard halving calculation gives R=(X_R,Y_R) with
X_R=D(A+p)(C+q)/r²,
Y_R=−D(A+p)(C+q)(Aq+Cp)/r³=−X_R(Aq+Cp)/r,
and 2R=Z. Indeed X_R−A²=A(C+q)(Aq+Cp)/r² and X_R+C²=C(A+p)(Aq+Cp)/r². Their product with X_R is Y_R², and substitution into duplication gives x(2R)=D²/r² and y(2R)=−D²pq/r³. Equivalently X_R=x(Z)+ρ0ρ1+ρ1ρ2+ρ2ρ0 and Y_R=−(ρ0+ρ1)(ρ1+ρ2)(ρ2+ρ0). These identities use p²−r²=A², q²+r²=C² and C²−A²=B² only.
Thus δ(R) can be computed directly as
([D(A+p)(C+q)], [A(C+q)(Aq+Cp)], [C(A+p)(Aq+Cp)]).
The original equality D_n=T0+2nP implies R−nP∈E(Q)[2]. At the rank2 base, write δ(R) in the ordered mod2 basis (T0,Ta,P,U). Its U coefficient must be zero and its P coefficient must be n mod2. Adding any 2-torsion point to R changes neither of these coefficients. Hence this necessary test is independent of the chosen half and requires no odd-prime saturation.
Equivalently D_n must lie in T0+4E(Q) if n is even, or T0+2P+4E(Q) if n is odd. Of the four abstract free-coordinate cosets inside T0+2E modulo4E, the original orbit allows the two with U half-parity zero. This is a group-coset statement. We have not shown that this condition excludes any rational point already surviving the full four-factor cover, and we do not claim an independent factor-of-two saving on physical points. Controls with R=U or P+U fail this necessary group test but are not asserted to satisfy the paired geometric receiver. Signed n=±3,±4,±5 and every torsion choice pass their corresponding parity test.
6. Rationally surjective étale four-root cover
For the forced c, define C_four by c u1²=F1, c u2²=F2, c u3²=F3, c u4²=F4. Its map to the retained genus9 cover is λ=c u1u2, μ=c u3u4, ω=c u2u4. Every strict rational point of the genus9 cover lifts over Q: choose u4²=F4/c and recover u2=ω/(c u4), u1=λ/(c u2), u3=μ/(c u4). The common-class theorem ensures these roots are rational and nonzero; the formulas preserve all three signed products.
Geometrically the four factor squareclasses are independent over Qbar(α), with eight distinct finite branch values and no branch at infinity. The connected degree16 cover has 2g−2=−32+8·8=32, so genus17. The genus9 subcover corresponds to the even-weight span generated by F1F2, F3F4, F2F4. Adjoining F4/c gives a degree2 cover; the same eight branch values already have ramification index2 below, and infinity has even valuation. The relative cover is étale, as also checked by 2·(2·9−2)=2·17−2.
This is rational surjectivity on the strict nonbranch locus, not a birational identification, not a lowering of genus, and not an effectivity or emptiness theorem. α=1 boundaries lift on both parity orientations and remain nonphysical.
