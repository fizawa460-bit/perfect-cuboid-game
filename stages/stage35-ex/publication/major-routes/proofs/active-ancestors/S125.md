125.1 Main result


Section124 made the family Lang constant explicit:


  hat h(R) >= (1/400) delta,


where


  delta=log|Delta_min(E)|.


This section avoids expanding the suppressed O(log Delta) terms in Everest-McLaren-Ward Section5.


The key extra family-specific fact is that


  R=2P3.


Therefore every multiple nR lies in 2E(Q), so its full rational 2-Kummer class is trivial.


Using only this trivial Kummer class, Silverman's explicit height-difference bound, and the elementary nonprimitive-term inequality of Everest-McLaren-Ward Lemma3.3, we obtain directly:


  boxed:
  every denominator EDS term with index n>=296 has a primitive divisor.


Equivalently the Zsigmondy bound for the retained R-sequence satisfies


  boxed:
  Z_B<=295.


Combining with Section123:


  M=max(|n|,|k|)+1


is a defective index on GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE. Hence


  boxed:
  max(|n|,|k|)<=294.


This is an explicit finite index range independent of t.


125.2 Denominator notation and Kummer triviality


Let


  mathcal B_n


denote the denominator of x(nR) in lowest terms in the global minimal model:


  x(nR)=A_n/mathcal B_n.


The retained root denominator C_n satisfies


  mathcal B_n=C_n^2.


Thus primitive prime divisors of mathcal B_n and C_n are the same.


Because R=2P3,


  nR in 2E(Q)


for every n>=1.


B even.


The displayed model is global minimal:


  y^2=x(x-A^2)(x+C^2).


Trivial full-2 Kummer class gives rational squares


  x(nR),
  x(nR)-A^2,
  x(nR)+C^2.


Write in lowest terms


  x(nR)=p^2/q^2,


with gcd(p,q)=1 and p,q>0. Since nR is nontorsion and lies in the real identity component,


  p>q


and


  x(nR)-A^2=r^2/q^2


for an integer r>=0.


Hence


  p^2-r^2=A^2 q^2.


B odd.


Use the retained 2-minimal model


  y^2+xy
  =x^3+((B^2-1)/4)x^2-(A^2C^2/16)x.


After the harmless completion


  y' = y+x/2,


the x-coordinate is unchanged and


  y'^2=x(x-A^2/4)(x+C^2/4).


Since A is divisible by 4, A/2 and C/2 are rational with A/2 integral, and the trivial 2-Kummer class gives


  x(nR)=p^2/q^2,
  x(nR)-A^2/4=r^2/q^2.


Thus


  p^2-r^2=(A/2)^2 q^2.


In both parity models define


  a0=A       if B is even,
  a0=A/2     if B is odd.


Then


  p^2-r^2=a0^2 q^2.


Moreover


  mathcal B_n=q^2.


125.3 Direct denominator recovery from naive height


Factor


  (p-r)(p+r)=a0^2 q^2.


For every nondegenerate primitive Pythagorean triple a0>=2. Since both factors are positive integers,


  2p=(p-r)+(p+r)
     <=1+a0^2q^2
     <2a0^2q^2.


Hence


  p<=a0^2 q^2.


Since p>q,


  h(x(nR))=2log p.


Therefore


  h(x(nR))
  <=4log a0+4log q
  =4log a0+2log mathcal B_n.


So the exact family-specific denominator lower bound is


  boxed:
  log mathcal B_n
  >=(1/2)h(x(nR))-2log a0.


Since a0<=A<=C,


  log mathcal B_n
  >=(1/2)h(x(nR))-2log C.


This is the family-specific replacement for the generic denominator lower bounds in Everest-McLaren-Ward Section5.


125.4 Uniform minimal-discriminant lower bounds


Write


  L=log C,
  delta=log|Delta_min|.


For the primitive Euclid parametrization:


B even:
  A=m^2-n0^2,
  B=2mn0,
  C=m^2+n0^2.


Then


  A=(m-n0)(m+n0)>sqrt(C/2),


so


  Delta_even=16A^4C^4(A^2+C^2)^2
  >=4C^10.


Hence


  delta>=10L+log4.


B odd:
  A=2mn0,
  B=m^2-n0^2,
  C=m^2+n0^2.


Then


  A>sqrt(2C),


so


  Delta_odd=A^4C^4(A^2+C^2)^2/256
  >=C^10/64.


Hence


  delta>=10L-6log2.


Since C>=5, both cases give


  boxed:
  delta>11.


Retain from Section124


  h(j)<=12L+log6912.


125.5 Two explicit height-to-denominator inequalities


Silverman's full-x-height inequality is


  -h(j)/4-delta/6-1.946
  <=hat h(Q)-h(Q)
  <=h(j)/6+delta/6+2.14.


Lower denominator.


The right-hand inequality gives


  h(x(nR))
  >=n^2 hat h(R)-h(j)/6-delta/6-2.14.


Insert this into Section125.3:


  log mathcal B_n
  >=(1/2)n^2 hat h(R)
    -[h(j)/12+delta/12+1.07+2L].


Using


  h(j)<=12L+log6912


and the two lower bounds for delta above, the bracket is at most delta for every C>=5.


Therefore


  boxed:
  log mathcal B_n
  >=(1/2)n^2 hat h(R)-delta.          (LB)


Upper denominator.


The left-hand Silverman inequality gives


  h(x(mR))
  <=m^2 hat h(R)+h(j)/4+delta/6+1.946.


Again the same elementary L,delta bounds show


  h(j)/4+delta/6+1.946<=delta


for every primitive specialization.


Since the denominator height never exceeds the full x-height,


  boxed:
  log mathcal B_m
  <=m^2 hat h(R)+delta.               (UB)


These two inequalities are uniform over both primitive parity orientations.


125.6 Nonprimitive-term inequality


Everest-McLaren-Ward Lemma3.3 states:


if mathcal B_n has no primitive divisor, then


  mathcal B_n
  divides
  product_(p|n) p^2 mathcal B_(n/p).


Taking logarithms gives


  log mathcal B_n
  <=eta(n)+sum_(p|n) log mathcal B_(n/p),


where


  eta(n)=2 sum_(p|n) log p.


Apply (UB):


  log mathcal B_n
  <=eta(n)
    +n^2 rho(n) hat h(R)
    +omega(n) delta,


where


  rho(n)=sum_(p|n) 1/p^2


and omega(n) is the number of distinct prime divisors.


Combine with (LB):


  n^2(1/2-rho(n))hat h(R)
  <=eta(n)+(omega(n)+1)delta.        (*)


125.7 Insert the explicit family Lang bound


From Section124,


  hat h(R)>=delta/400.


Everest-McLaren-Ward records the uniform elementary bounds


  rho(n)<=0.453,
  omega(n)<=1.443 log n.


Also


  eta(n)<=2log n.


Since delta>11, inequality (*) for a defective term implies


  (47/400000)n^2
  <=eta(n)/delta+omega(n)+1
  <(2/11+1.443)log n+1
  <1.625 log n+1.                    (**)


At n=296,


  left side
  =47*296^2/400000
  =10.29488,


whereas


  1.625 log296+1
  <10.247.


Thus (**) fails at n=296.


The difference


  F(n)
  =(47/400000)n^2-1.625log n-1


has derivative


  F'(n)
  =(94/400000)n-1.625/n,


which is positive for every n>=84.


Therefore (**) fails for every


  n>=296.


Conclusion:


  boxed:
  mathcal B_n has a primitive divisor for every n>=296.


Hence


  boxed:
  Z_B<=295.

125.14 Parity refinement — final numeric authority for Section125


The bound Z_B<=295 above is valid but not optimal even within the same argument.


For odd n, Everest-McLaren-Ward records the stronger estimate


  rho(n)<=0.203.


Thus a defective odd term satisfies


  (297/400000)n^2
  <1.625log n+1.


At n=109,


  (297/400000)*109^2
  >8.82,


while


  1.625log109+1
  <8.63.


The difference is increasing for n>=34, so every odd n>=109 has a primitive divisor.


Therefore every odd defective index is at most 107.


From Section125.7 every index n>=296, regardless of parity, has a primitive divisor, so every even defective index is at most 294.


Hence the final Section125 bound is


  boxed:
  Z_B<=294.


Accordingly, the GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE physical defect index


  M=max(|n|,|k|)+1


satisfies


  M<=294,


and therefore


  boxed:
