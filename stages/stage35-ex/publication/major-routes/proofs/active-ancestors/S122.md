122.1 Setup

122.2 The reciprocal involution produces an unconditional partner on E


Define


  P_tau=
  (
    alpha/s^2,
    -alpha p q/s^3
  ).


This point lies on E because


  x(P_tau)=alpha/s^2=(a/s)^2,


  x(P_tau)-alpha
  =alpha(1-s^2)/s^2
  =alpha p^2/s^2
  =(a p/s)^2,


  x(P_tau)+1
  =(alpha+s^2)/s^2
  =q^2/s^2.


Thus P_tau has trivial full-2 Kummer class and, exactly,


  P_tau in 2E_t(Q).


This construction is unconditional once the physical Branch-B roots exist. It does not assume GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE or specialized primitivity.


Put


  Q_tau=T0+P_tau.


Using translation by T0,


  Q_tau=(-s^2,-s p q).


Therefore the reciprocal partner is obtained by the exact root swap


  (r,s,p,q) -> (s,r,q,p).


The swapped packet again satisfies the full physical square system:


  beta-s^2=r^2,
  alpha+s^2=q^2,
  1-s^2=p^2.


So Q_tau is itself a physical-compatible Branch-B E3 point at the same rational parameter t.

122.6 Paired physical indices on GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE


Assume now the conditional GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE branch and write


  P_tau=kR,
  k=m-n.


Then


  Q_tau=T0+kR=Q_k,


so the reciprocal involution pairs two physical indices


  n <-> k.


Their roots are swapped:

  (r,s,p,q),

  m=n+k.


The pair satisfies the exact reciprocal-x equation

  1/x(nR)+1/x(kR)=1/x(R).


Indeed


  1/x(nR)=r^2/alpha,

  x(nR)x(kR)

  boxed:
  (x(nR)-x(R))(x(kR)-x(R))=x(R)^2.


This is the exact paired-index receiver.


122.7 The reciprocal pairing has no rational fixed point


If


  k=n,


then


  x(kR)=x(nR),


hence


  r^2=s^2.

  r^2+s^2=beta=b^2

  (b/r)^2=2,


impossible over Q.


If


  k=-n,


then x(kR)=x(nR) again, giving the same contradiction.


Therefore


  k != n,
  k != -n.


Equivalently,


  m != 2n,
  m != 0.

By the retained nonboundary Branch-B index classification, the reciprocal partner is another nonboundary physical-compatible point and therefore inherits the same current index restriction

Thus GENERIC_IMAGE_MULTIPLE solutions occur in fixed-point-free reciprocal pairs of distinct, nonopposite physical indices.
