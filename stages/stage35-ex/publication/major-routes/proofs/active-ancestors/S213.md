## 1. O/T0 endpoint reductions excluded without cyclic Q

Exact opposite X means that if Q reduces to O, then Qsharp reduces to O
too: both X valuations are negative. If Q reduces to T0, its X valuation
is positive (T0 is smooth and X=0 is its only finite reduced point), hence
Qsharp also reduces to T0. The same statements hold starting from Qsharp.
H-side singular node reductions are already excluded by S202; no singular
affine point is treated as a group element.

Suppose both endpoints reduce to J in {O,T0}. Since d is odd,
dJ=J, so (213.1) and dQ=mP give
m*barP=J, 2nd*barP=T0, and 2m*barP=O.               (213.2)
Let t=ord(barP). The last equality gives t|2m. Because m is ODD, the
2-primary part of t is at most2. Every EVEN multiple of barP therefore has
odd order: its order divides t/gcd(t,2), an odd integer. But 2nd*barP=T0
has order2, a contradiction. This arbitrary-t proof uses no finite bound,
gcd(d,M), cyclic Q, or smallest-prime hypothesis.

Therefore Q,Qsharp are finite nonsingular points with NONZERO reduced X:

v_ell(X(Q))=v_ell(X(Qsharp))=0, v_ell(rho)=0.        (213.3)

Their reduced X values are opposite and nonzero at odd ell, so they are
distinct and barD_n=barQ-barQsharp is NOT O. Consequently X(D_n) is
integral; C is a unit; and every r,s,p,qroot is ell-integral by the four
exact square identities. This removes the possible negative-root-valuation
escape at EVERY G/H denominator endpoint, not only the least one.

This makes Q cyclic CONDITIONAL ON THE S_BOUNDARY, even if d is not coprime
