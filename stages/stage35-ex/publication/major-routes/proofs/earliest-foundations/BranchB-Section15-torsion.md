15.3 Uniform exclusion of Z/8 on E'


Let phihat:E'->E be the dual 2-isogeny, whose kernel is generated by the unique rational order-two point (0,0). If E'(Q) contained a point T of order 8, then 4T=(0,0), hence phihat(T) would be a rational point of order 4 on
E:
y^2=x(x-a^2)(x+1).


A rational order-four point on E would have to halve one of the three nonzero rational 2-torsion points. The points (0,0) and (-1,0) cannot be halved because the full-2 halving criterion would require respectively -a^2 or -1 to be a rational square. The remaining point (a^2,0) can be halved only if
h^2=1+a^2
for some h in Q.


But b^2=1-a^2, so
b^2, 1, h^2
would be three rational squares in arithmetic progression with common difference a^2. Dividing by the nonzero rational square a^2 gives a nonconstant three-square arithmetic progression with common difference 1. This contradicts the retained Fermat congruent-number input that 1 is not a congruent number.


Therefore
E_PRIME_SPECIALIZED_Z8_TORSION_POSSIBLE=false
for every rational t>1.


15.4 Uniform exclusion of Z/12 on E' via a global E3 3-torsion obstruction


If E'(Q) contained a point T of order 12, then 6T=(0,0), so phihat(T) would be a rational point of order 6 on E. In particular E would contain nonzero rational 3-torsion. We now exclude rational 3-torsion on E uniformly for every rational nonzero a.


Write alpha=a^2. For
E:
y^2=x^3+(1-alpha)x^2-alpha x,
the 3-division polynomial is
psi_3(x)=3x^4+4(1-alpha)x^3-6alpha x^2-alpha^2.


Regarded as a quadratic in alpha, its discriminant is
16x^3(x+1)^3.
For a rational 3-torsion x-coordinate x not equal to 0 or -1, rational alpha therefore forces
q^2=x(x+1)
for some q in Q.


Put
r=q/(x+1).
Then r is rational, r is not 0 or +/-1, and
x=r^2/(1-r^2),
q=r/(1-r^2).
Solving psi_3=0 for alpha gives the two branches
alpha_+(r)=-r^3(r+2)/((r-1)(r+1)^3),
alpha_-(r)=-r^3(r-2)/((r-1)^3(r+1)).
They are interchanged by r -> -r.


Consider alpha_+. Since alpha=a^2 is a rational square and
alpha_+(r)
=
(r^2/(r+1)^2) *
[-r(r+2)/((r-1)(r+1))],
there must be z in Q with
z^2=-r(r+2)/(r^2-1).
Equivalently
(z^2+1)r^2+2r-z^2=0.
For rational r, the discriminant of this quadratic must be a rational square:
4(z^4+z^2+1)=square.
Hence
w^2=z^4+z^2+1
for some w in Q.


The retained Mordell quartic source lock used earlier in Stage35EX states that the primitive integer equation

Thus for every rational a != 0,
E(Q)[3]=0.
For rational t>1 we have a>0, hence E has no rational 3-torsion and therefore E' cannot have rational 12-torsion.


Therefore
