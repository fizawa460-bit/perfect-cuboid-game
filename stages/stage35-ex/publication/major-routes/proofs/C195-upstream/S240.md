3. Every odd prime of H is split, and is 1 modulo8
Fix an odd prime ℓ|H. It is disjoint from ABC: if it divided A or C, primitivity would fail; if it divided B, H and C²−A² would force 2A²≡0. Put i=C/A moduloℓ. Then i²=−1 and (B/A)²=−2.
The reduced curve is
Ebar:y²=x(x−A²)², with split node (A²,0).
Its nonsingular part is parametrized by τ=y/(x−A²), x=τ², with O represented by τ=∞. The group coordinate is
u=(τ+A)/(τ−A), O↦1, T0↦−1.                           (240.3)
The chord group law becomes multiplication of u. Reduction on the nonsingular subgroup is a homomorphism; the split node's two branches τ=±A are omitted. P reduces nonsingularly since AC≠A² moduloℓ. Consequently all original D_n reduce in this subgroup, possibly to O, never to the node.
For P, (τ(P)/A)²=i. Thus τ(P)/A has order8 in F_ℓ*, proving ℓ≡1 modulo8. In particular -1 and2 are squares locally. This proves the prime property using the actual rational orbit point, not merely H being a sum of two squares.
Direct doubling gives R=(D²/B²,−D³/B³). Its group coordinate simplifies to
ρ=u(R)=(C+B)/(C−B),
ρ+ρ⁻¹=−6, u(P)²=ρ.                                  (240.4)
The denominators in (240.3)-(240.4) are nonzero in the stated nonsingular charts. Let h=ord_Fℓ*(ρ). Then h>2, h divides (ℓ−1)/2, and
u(D_n)=−ρ^n.                                         (240.5)
All dependence on signed n in the reduced original orbit is now exact finite multiplication, rather than sampling elliptic coordinates.
4. Complete local s classification, including the boundary lifts
Write σ_n=x(D_n)+B². There are three cases for each ℓ|H.
Case I: ρ^n=−1. D_n reduces to O. In the formal parameter z=−x/y, x=z⁻² times a unit in1+ℓZ_ℓ, so σ_n is a Q_ℓ square. This is the denominator class, treated in Section5.
Case II: ρ^n≠−1 and n is not ±1 modulo h. The finite point chart gives
σ_n/A² ≡ F(u)/(u−1)² (modℓ),
F(u)=−u²+6u−1, u=−ρ^n,
F(−ρ^n)=−(ρ^n−ρ)(ρ^n−ρ⁻¹).                           (240.6)
Here F(u)≠0. Hence σ_n is a Q_ℓ square exactly when F(−ρ^n) is a nonzero quadratic residue moduloℓ. A nonsquare excludes the entire integer n class modulo h. This is an exact unit criterion; no higher precision is needed in this case.
Case III: n≡ε modulo h with ε=±1. This is the zero-reduction boundary of σ; declaring it locally soluble from its zero residue would be incorrect. Put
Z_ℓpoint=[h]R, z_h=−x(Z_ℓpoint)/y(Z_ℓpoint),
d_ℓ=v_ℓ(z_h)≥1, δ_ℓ=−2BD z_h∈Q*, v_ℓ(δ_ℓ)=d_ℓ,
j=(n−ε)/h.
For n≠ε, there is the exact square-unit factorization
σ_n=ε δ_ℓ j V_n, V_n∈1+ℓZ_ℓ.                         (240.7)
Thus σ_n is a Q_ℓ square iff εδ_ℓj is. Equivalently, d_ℓ+v_ℓ(j) must be even and the normalized unit of εδ_ℓj must be a square moduloℓ. This covers unbounded v_ℓ(n−ε).
Proof of the lift. Let S=T0+R=(-B²,-BD). The exact translated formal function from Section239 is
f(z)=x(S+T(z))+B²=−2BDz+(B⁴−D²)z²+2B³Dz³+z⁴Z_ℓ[[z]].
Here its linear coefficient -2BD is a unit. For l=L(z_h), l/z_h∈1+ℓZ_ℓ and v_ℓ(l)=d_ℓ. Section239's integral differential derivation gives k![w^k]f(Exp(w))∈Z_ℓ. After division by the leading term -2BD j l, every term of degree k≥2 has valuation at least (k−1)d_ℓ−v_ℓ(k!)≥1. The bounds tend to infinity, including after factoring j; arbitrarily divisible j causes no loss. Hence f(Exp(jl))=(-2BD)j z_h times a unit in1+ℓZ_ℓ. This proves ε=1. For ε=-1 use x(D_{-n})=x(D_n), replacing j by -j. Since ℓ≡1 mod8, the sign is a local square, but it is retained in (240.7).
d_ℓ is the formal depth of [h]R, not v_ℓ(H). They need not agree: for (A,B,C)=(165,52,173), v41(H)=2 but d41=1. The theorem keeps the actual formal depth; no bounded sample is used to assert d_ℓ=1 universally.

5. Exact discriminant-prime denominator/order adapter

For odd h it never happens. If a physical common denominator b exists as in the retained packet, this is exactly its ℓ-denominator event.
Let g=ord(u(P)); h=g/gcd(g,2). When h is even, g=2h=4d with d=h/2. Equation(240.8) is n/d odd. 7. Infinite exact CRT survivors even after every bad prime is included
Let S be the primes dividing 2ABC H. It contains every bad-reduction prime because Δ(E)=16A⁴C⁴H². For each odd ℓ|H compute h_ℓ,d_ℓ,δ_ℓ as above, and put K=lcm(h_ℓ). Choose a positive integer M divisible by K with the following exact prime exponents:
* v2(M) is odd and at least max(5,v2(K)).
* At each odd p dividing ABC, v_p(M) is even and at least max(2,v_p(K)).
* At each odd ℓ dividing H, v_ℓ(M) is at least max(1,v_ℓ(K)) and has the same parity as d_ℓ.
* At other primes dividing K, keep exponent v_p(K).
The sets of odd base and discriminant primes are disjoint, so these choices are compatible. Cross-prime divisibility between different orders h_ℓ is explicitly absorbed by v_p(K); no order coprimality or independence is assumed.
Choose a positive integer t by CRT, with t a unit at every prime of S. Specify its residues by making the following units equal1 in the indicated residue fields:
At2: η(M/2^{v2(M)})t≡1 mod8, η=-1 for B even,+1 for B odd.
At odd p dividing AC: 2(M/p^{v_p(M)})t≡1 mod p.
At odd p dividing B: -2(M/p^{v_p(M)})t≡1 mod p.
At odd ℓ dividing H: (δ_ℓ/ℓ^{d_ℓ})(M/ℓ^{v_ℓ(M)})t/h_ℓ≡1 modℓ.
Every coefficient is a unit. In particular h_ℓ divides ℓ−1 and is an ℓ-adic unit. The CRT moduli8 and the distinct odd primes are coprime. Let T=8∏_{odd p∈S}p; t=t0+kT gives infinitely many positive choices. Set
n=1+M t.                                             (240.9)
For the base primes, n²−1=Mt(2+Mt); the exact parity and unit choices above give the Section238/239 local s squareconditions, with the negative sign at B-primes. At a discriminant prime n≡1 mod h_ℓ, j=Mt/h_ℓ. Formula(240.7) gives even valuation d_ℓ+v_ℓ(M) and unit square by construction. Consequently the ACTUAL σ_n on the original orbit is locally square at every prime of S, for every primitive base. It is never in a discriminant-prime denominator class: n≡1 mod h_ℓ and h_ℓ>2.
This is a universal construction, not extrapolation from sample CRT hits. It does not assert that σ_n is a rational square; it can fail at a good prime outside S. The script checks the constructor on all ten bases and four t values in each family without evaluating the huge elliptic coordinates.
8. The original strict real chamber also leaves infinitely many indices
The above partial receiver cannot be emptied by adding only the original real sign/positivity conditions. For a fixed base, the progression (240.9) has the form n=n0+kMT. R lies on E(R)'s identity component: P has x=AC>A² and belongs to that component, and R=2P. This component is a real circle group. Integrating its invariant differential gives the additive circle coordinate; a rational circle angle would make the point torsion. Since R has infinite order, the nonzero multiple [MT]R also has irrational angle and its successive multiples are dense in this circle. Every nonempty open arc is visited infinitely often for positive k.
Translation by T0 takes it to the other real component, whose finite x interval is [-C²,0]. The arc -B²<x<0, y<0 is nonempty and open. Hence infinitely many n in the same exact CRT progression satisfy BOTH original strict real inequalities. For them r,p,q are positive rational by (240.2) and σ_n>0 with local s squareclass trivial at every prime in S.
This still does not construct rational s or Q, nor verify Q's infinite quotient class. It proves nonemptiness of a precisely stated PARTIAL receiver, even with the real chamber retained. No rational-point or physical-packet existence credit follows. No quantitative density or effective first-index bound is claimed.
