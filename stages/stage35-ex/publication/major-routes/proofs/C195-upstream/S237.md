2. Original orbit depth, including the previously unused dyadic case
Direct doubling gives 2P=((D/B)²,−(D/B)³), S*=T0+2P=(-B²,−BD), and W=4P with
x(W)=F²/(4D² B²), F=A⁴−A²C²+C⁴=D²+B⁴.                         (237.1)
For every prime ℓ|D let e=vℓ(D)>0. Primitivity gives ℓ∤B, so F is an ℓ-unit. On the displayed integral Weierstrass model the formal parameter z=−x/y of W has depth
t0=e for odd ℓ; t0=e+1 for ℓ=2.                               (237.2)
Indeed x(W) has valuation −2e or −2e−2, respectively, and the leading x³ term forces vℓ(y(W))=3 vℓ(x(W))/2. The formal group at O of an integral Weierstrass equation has integral addition coefficients. Thus its filtration z∈ℓ^t0 Zℓ is closed under all signed integer multiples. No minimal-model or valuation-preserving logarithm assumption is needed.
For odd n=2j+1 write D_n=S*+[j]W. If V=[j]W=(x,y) is finite of depth t>=t0, the EXACT rational chord identity, rederived in Section230, is
x(S*+V)+B²=[(B⁴−D²)x+2BDy+B²(D²+B⁴)]/(x+B²)².               (237.3)
It follows by expanding the slope (y+BD)/(x+B²) and eliminating y²=x³+B²x²−D²x. Since vℓ(x)=−2t and vℓ(y)=−3t, the three numerator contributions after division have lower valuations 2t, e+vℓ(2)+t, and 4t. Hence
odd ℓ: vℓ(x(D_n)+B²)>=2e and vℓ(x(D_n))=0;
ℓ=2: v2(x(D_n)+B²)>=2e+2 and v2(x(D_n))=0.                   (237.4)
The case V=O gives S* and satisfies the depth bound with infinite valuation; s=0 then fails the physical positivity mask. All signed j are covered.
For even n=2j, D_n=T0+[j]W, and the exact torsion translation is x(D_n)=−D²/x([j]W). For finite V this gives
odd ℓ: vℓ(x(D_n))=2e+2t>=4e;
ℓ=2: v2(x(D_n))=2e+2t>=4e+2.                               (237.5)
If V=O then D_n=T0 has r=0 and is nonphysical. The bounds use the original n orbit only, not dependence between Q and P.
3. NEW exact valuation locking at every prime of AC
Because b is coprime to D, root and numerator valuations agree at every ℓ|D.
For odd n, (237.4) gives r an ℓ-unit and vℓ(s)>=e (at 2, >=e+1). At ℓ|A, p is a unit and vℓ(q)>=e by q²=A²+s². At ℓ|C, q is a unit and vℓ(p)>=e by p²=C²−s². Consequently both pq+rs and pq−rs have valuation >=e. Their product is D², so BOTH valuations are EXACTLY e.
For even n, (237.5) gives vℓ(r)>=2e (at 2, >=2e+1), and s is a unit. At ℓ|A, vℓ(p)=e and q is a unit; at ℓ|C, vℓ(q)=e and p is a unit. The pq term has valuation e while rs has strictly larger valuation, so again both pq±rs have valuation exactly e.
Therefore, uniformly for both orientations and both signed n parities,
vℓ(J)=vℓ(K)=vℓ(AC) for EVERY prime ℓ|AC.                    (237.6)
If 2∤AC there is no dyadic support to lock. If 2|AC, A is the even primitive leg and v2(A)>=2; the above dyadic argument applies. In particular Section236's s<=e−2 alternative, although allowed by the four-root equations alone, is impossible on the retained original n orbit, for ALL e, not just e=2 or3.

6. NEW mandatory rational boundary point; correction to local-empty targeting
For EVERY retained primitive base, (237.10)-(237.11) have the explicit rational point
α=1, λ=B, μ=B, ω=A+C.                                     (237.12)
