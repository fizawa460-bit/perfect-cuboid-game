## 2. What remains after the original-base classification

The original-base rank-one theorem does **not** control the Mordell-Weil rank after a finite base change. A finite cover of the `k`-line can create new sections; equivalently a multisection on the original elliptic surface can become a section after base change.

Therefore the following mechanisms were intentionally left open by the R504 audit:

1. a low-degree rational base change producing a new non-torsion section;
2. an explicitly controlled multisection becoming a section after such a base change;
3. a uniform count that aggregates points coming from growing multiples rather than one fixed section.

A bounded primary-source/repository search confirms that these are real mathematical mechanisms in the general theory of elliptic K3/twist surfaces, but it did not supply a theorem already adapted to this exact symmetric-`k` receiver with all of the following simultaneously:

- exact Stage19 primitive/canonical population;
- physical cutoff by the integral space diagonal;
- exactly-two-face postfilter;
- bounded multiplicity into primitive canonical boxes;
- a polynomially large bounded-height family strong enough to beat `B^(1/4)`.

The absence statement here is deliberately narrow: **no ready same-measure adapter was found in the bounded search**. It is not a theorem that no useful base change exists.

## 3. Why another repo-native fixed-section mutation is not justified

On the original base, the hostile-audited rank-one classification already exhausts independent `Q(k)` sections: every free section is a multiple of the generator. Fixed higher multiples increase the rational-function complexity and do not produce a lighter independent section. Thus repeating fixed-multiple algebra on the same base is not a new route.

A successful continuation now needs genuinely new input of one of the following forms:

- an explicit low-degree base change with a new section and a full Stage19 height/multiplicity adapter;
- a quantitative theorem for suitable multisections/base changes under physical height;
- a new explicit parametrization not already contained in the original-base section lattice.

Such a construction would reopen R504. If it is mathematically distinct from the present route allocation, it may also justify a new persistent route ID beginning at R508.
