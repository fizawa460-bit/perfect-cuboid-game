## 1. Symmetric-k receiver

For rational `k,t,z` with

\[
t^4+1=(k^4+1)z^2,
\]

the shared-edge construction

\[
e=2kpq,\qquad x=k^2p^2-q^2,\qquad y=k^2q^2-p^2,
\]

with `t=q/p` satisfies

\[
e^2+x^2=(k^2p^2+q^2)^2,
\]
\[
e^2+y^2=(k^2q^2+p^2)^2,
\]
and
\[
e^2+x^2+y^2=(k^4+1)(p^4+q^4).
\]

Thus the quartic receiver gives two integral face diagonals and an integral space diagonal after clearing denominators.

## 2. Jacobian and isotrivial twist

Put

\[
F=k^4+1.
\]

The quartic fiber maps to

\[
E_F:\quad Y^2=X^3-4F^2X
\]

by

\[
X=-\frac{4t^2}{z^2},\qquad
Y=\frac{4t(t^4-1)}{z^3}.
\]

The obvious section `(t,z)=(k,1)` maps to

\[
P(k)=(-4k^2,\ 4k(k^4-1)).
\]

The curve `E_F` is the quadratic twist by `F` of the constant curve

\[
E_0:\quad v^2=u^3-4u.
\]

The quadratic cover

\[
C:\quad s^2=k^4+1
\]

is itself birational over `Q` to `E_0` via

\[
u=\frac{2(s+1)}{k^2},\qquad
v=\frac{4(s+1)}{k^3},
\]

with rational inverse `k=2u/v` away from the usual exceptional points.

Let `T=(0,0)` on `E_0`. Under this birational identification the deck involution `s -> -s` acts as

\[
Q\longmapsto T-Q.
\]

A standard quadratic-twist descent identifies the free part of `E_F(Q(k))` with the anti-invariant nonconstant maps `C -> E_0`. Since `C` is `Q`-isomorphic to `E_0`, those maps modulo translations are `End_Q(E_0)`. The curve has `j=1728`; its extra CM endomorphism is defined only after adjoining `i`, so

\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(E_0)=\mathbf Z.
\]

For a map `[n]Q+S`, anti-invariance requires

\[
nT+2S=0.
\]

The nonconstant coefficient therefore has even parity. The first nonzero descended homomorphism class is coefficient `2`. The section `P(k)` above is exactly that primitive descended class, up to sign and rational 2-torsion. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}E_F(\mathbf Q(k))=1}
\]

and its free part is generated by `P(k)`.

This is the key R504 classification: there is no second independent `Q(k)`-rational section hidden on the original symmetric-k surface.

## 3. The first physical nondegenerate section is the 3P section

The `P` section corresponds to the obvious quartic point `t=k`, which gives `x=0` and is physically degenerate. The reciprocal obvious section similarly makes `y=0`. The doubled Jacobian section has positive `X` on the positive real base, whereas every real quartic image has

\[
X=-\frac{4Ft^2}{t^4+1}<0,
\]

so it does not produce a positive real quartic point.

The first nondegenerate real rational section is the already accepted third-multiple section

\[
t_3(k)=\frac{k(k^8-6k^4-3)}{3k^8+6k^4-1},
\]
\[
z_3(k)=\frac{k^{16}+28k^{12}+6k^8+28k^4+1}
{(3k^8+6k^4-1)^2},
\]

which satisfies identically

\[
t_3(k)^4+1=(k^4+1)z_3(k)^2.
\]

## 4. Homogenized 3P Stage19 family

Write `k=u/v` in lowest positive terms and define

\[
A=u^8-6u^4v^4-3v^8,
\]
\[
B=3u^8+6u^4v^4-v^8,
\]
\[
C=u^{16}+28u^{12}v^4+6u^8v^8+28u^4v^{12}+v^{16}.
\]

Then `t_3=uA/(vB)`. Clearing the common `v^2` denominator from the physical box gives

\[
E=2u^2v^2AB,
\]
\[
X=u^2v^2(B^2-A^2),
\]
\[
Y=u^4A^2-v^4B^2,
\]
\[
D=(u^4+v^4)C.
\]

The two face diagonals are

\[
H_X=u^2v^2(A^2+B^2),
\qquad
H_Y=u^4A^2+v^4B^2,
\]

and exact expansion gives

\[
E^2+X^2=H_X^2,
\qquad
E^2+Y^2=H_Y^2,
\qquad
E^2+X^2+Y^2=D^2.
\]

## 5. Exact primitive gcd of the 3P family

For coprime positive `u,v`,

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(E,X,Y)=2^{7[\,u,v\text{ both odd}\,]}\le128.
}
\]

For any odd common prime `ell`, `ell` cannot divide `u` or `v` because the other edge remains a unit modulo `ell`. Thus `ell|E` forces `ell|A` or `ell|B`; then `ell|X` forces `ell|A` and `ell|B`. But

\[
B-3A=8v^4(3u^4+v^4),
\]
\[
3B-A=8u^4(u^4+3v^4),
\]

and for an odd prime with `ell\nmid uv` these two congruences imply `ell|8`, impossible.

If `u,v` have opposite parity, one of `X,Y` is odd, so the gcd is odd and hence `1`. If both are odd, then modulo `32`,

\[
A\equiv-8,\qquad B\equiv8,
\]

so `v_2(A)=v_2(B)=3` and `v_2(E)=7`, while `X,Y` are divisible by at least `2^7`. Hence the displayed formula is exact.

Therefore primitive reduction cannot lower the degree-20 height by an unbounded factor.

## 6. Exact 3P height scale

Let `H=max(u,v)`. Since all coefficients of `C` are nonnegative,

\[
D=(u^4+v^4)C\ge H^{20}.
\]

Also, for `u,v<=H`,

\[
C\le64H^{16},\qquad u^4+v^4\le2H^4,
\]

hence

\[
D\le128H^{20}.
\]

After primitive reduction,

\[
\boxed{H^{20}/128\le D_{\rm prim}\le128H^{20}.}
\]

Thus the 3P family has exact parameter-height degree `20`.

## 7. Exactly-two exceptions are finite

Dehomogenize with `v=1`. The missing face satisfies

\[
X(k)^2+Y(k)^2=(k-1)^2(k+1)^2(k^2+1)^2Q_{32}(k),
\]

where

\[
Q_{32}=Q_1Q_2Q_3Q_4
\]

with

\[
Q_1=k^8+4k^6-8k^5+14k^4-8k^3+4k^2+1,
\]
\[
Q_2=k^8+4k^6+8k^5+14k^4+8k^3+4k^2+1,
\]
\[
Q_3=k^8-4k^7+4k^6+4k^5-2k^4-4k^3+4k^2+4k+1,
\]
\[
Q_4=k^8+4k^7+4k^6-4k^5-2k^4+4k^3+4k^2-4k+1.
\]

The deterministic verifier checks

\[
\gcd(Q_{32},Q_{32}')=1\pmod3.
\]

Hence `Q_32` is squarefree over `Q`. The smooth projective model

\[
w^2=Q_{32}(k)
\]

has degree `32` and genus `15`, so Faltings gives only finitely many rational parameters with a third square face.

There is a nonempty real open interval around `k=5/2` on which all three edges are positive and have a fixed strict order. Restricting to a compact rational subinterval of that open set preserves `gg H^2` reduced rational parameters of height at most `H`.

On that interval, the scale-invariant ratio `E/X` is the nonconstant rational function

\[
\frac{(k^8-6k^4-3)(3k^8+6k^4-1)}
{4(k-1)(k+1)(k^2+1)(k^4+1)(k^8+6k^4+1)},
\]

whose degree is at most `16`. Thus each primitive canonical box has at most `16` 3P parameters there.

Combining the exact height scale, bounded parameter multiplicity, and finite third-face exceptions gives the family-specific theorem

\[
\boxed{N_{R504,3P}(B)=\Theta(B^{1/10}).}
\]

This is a new explicit infinite Stage19 family, but it is weaker than the audited R501/R502 `B^(1/4)` lower and therefore does not improve the global Stage25 lower exponent.

## 8. What the rank-one classification does and does not close

The rank-one theorem removes a major hidden possibility: on the **original** symmetric-k elliptic surface there is no second independent `Q(k)`-rational section waiting to provide an unrelated lighter polynomial family.

Under the constant-curve description, the rational function obtained from a fixed odd multiple `[n]P` has degree growing quadratically with `|n|`; the 3P formula realizes the first nondegenerate case. Thus moving to fixed higher multiples increases, rather than decreases, rational parameter height. No fixed-multiple `Q(k)` section supplies a route to an exponent above the current global `1/4` lower.

This does **not** close every conceivable R504 mutation. A nontrivial finite base change can enlarge the Mordell-Weil group, and a low-degree multisection can become a new section only after such a base change. Uniform aggregation over growing multiples also requires a separate height argument before an exhaustive quantitative statement is allowed.
