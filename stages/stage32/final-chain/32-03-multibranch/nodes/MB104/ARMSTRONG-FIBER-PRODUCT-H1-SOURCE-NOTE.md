# Stage32 MB104 source note — Armstrong orbit-space H1 for the diagonal H quotient

Status: **EXTERNAL/STANDARD SOURCE ADAPTER / H1 METHOD ONLY / NO CONDUCTOR SIGN / NO CREDIT**

## Scope

This note records the standard topological input used to compute the first integral homology of the support-specific quotient

```text
X_H=(C8 x C8)/H_diag,
H=<s1,s2>~=(Z/2)^2.
```

It does not assert any Stage32 conductor transition, weighted-cut estimate, or endpoint credit by itself.

## 1. Armstrong orbit-space theorem

Let a discontinuous group `Gamma` act by homeomorphisms on a path-connected, simply connected, locally compact metric space `U`. Let `N` be the normal subgroup generated by all elements of `Gamma` having fixed points on `U`. Armstrong proves

```text
pi_1(U/Gamma) ~= Gamma/N.
```

Primary references:

- M. A. Armstrong, *On the fundamental group of an orbit space*, Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 61 (1965), 639-646, DOI `10.1017/S0305004100038974`.
- M. A. Armstrong, *The fundamental group of the orbit space of a discontinuous group*, Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 64 (1968), 299-301, DOI `10.1017/S0305004100042845`.

The 1968 statement applies to the universal-cover action used below. In particular, one must apply the theorem to the full deck/orbifold group on the simply connected universal cover, not merely to the finite group `H` acting on the non-simply-connected surface `C8 x C8`.

## 2. Orbifold group of one factor

For the retained `e=2` support-specific action, repository evidence gives

```text
C8/H ~= P1
```

with exactly eight order-two branch values: four with inertia `s1` and four with inertia `s2`; `s1*s2` is fixed-point-free.

The corresponding orientable orbifold group has presentation

```text
Delta = <x1,...,x8 |
         x1^2=...=x8^2=1,
         x1*x2*...*x8=1>,
```

with quotient map

```text
phi:Delta -> H,
phi(x1)=...=phi(x4)=s1,
phi(x5)=...=phi(x8)=s2.
```

Its torsion elements are precisely conjugates of nontrivial powers of the cone generators. Since all cone orders are two, the nontrivial elements with fixed points on the universal cover are the conjugates of the `x_i`.

This is the standard torsion theorem for cocompact Fuchsian/orbifold groups. Only the order-two special case is used here.

## 3. Diagonal quotient as a fiber product

Let `U` be the simply connected universal cover of `C8`. The simply connected universal cover of `C8 x C8` is `U x U`.

The group acting on `U x U` with coarse quotient `X_H` is the fiber product

```text
F = Delta x_H Delta
  = {(a,b) in Delta x Delta : phi(a)=phi(b)}.
```

Equivalently, if

```text
psi:Delta x Delta -> H,
psi(a,b)=phi(a)-phi(b),
```

then `F=ker(psi)` and `[Delta x Delta:F]=4`.

An element `(a,b) in F` fixes a point of `U x U` exactly when both `a` and `b` fix points in `U`. Therefore every nontrivial fixed element is represented by

```text
(g x_i g^-1, h x_j h^-1)
```

with `phi(x_i)=phi(x_j)`.

For abelianization it is enough to include, for each pair of branch generators of the same inertia type, four representatives indexed by the relative `H`-coset of the two conjugators. Kernel changes in either conjugator are themselves conjugations by elements of `F`, while simultaneous changes by the same `H`-coset do not change the relative class. Thus the finite representative table consists of

```text
2 inertia types * 4 branch generators * 4 branch generators * 4 relative H-cosets
= 128
```

fixed-element relations.

## 4. From coarse quotient to an A1 resolution

Repository evidence separately records that the singularities of the support-specific quotient are rational double points of type `A1`.

Replacing a sufficiently small `A1` singular neighborhood by its minimal-resolution neighborhood does not change integral `H_1`:

- the singular neighborhood is a cone on the link `RP3` and has `H_1=0`;
- the minimal-resolution neighborhood deformation retracts to the exceptional `P1` and has `H_1=0`;
- both are glued to the same exterior along the connected link `RP3`;
- the Mayer-Vietoris sequence therefore gives the same cokernel of `H_1(RP3)->H_1(exterior)` in degree one.

Doing this at all isolated `A1` points preserves `H_1`. Blowups of smooth projective surfaces also preserve `H_1`, so any smooth projective surface birational to the minimal resolution has the same first integral homology.

## 5. Consequence for Picard torsion when H1 vanishes

For a smooth projective complex surface `Y`, the universal coefficient theorem gives

```text
Tor H^2(Y,Z) ~= Tor H_1(Y,Z).
```

The exponential sequence identifies the first Chern class of a torsion line bundle with a torsion class in `H^2(Y,Z)` once `Pic^0(Y)=0`. Hence, if repository evidence gives `q(Y)=0` and the orbit-space computation gives

```text
H_1(Y,Z)=0,
```

then

```text
Pic^tau(Y)=0.
```

This kills the finite torsion ambiguity but does not by itself choose any residual conductor sheet.
