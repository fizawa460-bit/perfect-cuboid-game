## 1. Why q14 is triggered

Stage14-q13 classified the then-visible polynomial survivors as correlation problems and explicitly declined to manufacture an external search for the heavy primitive reciprocal-ray branch. Since then the heavy branch has changed mathematical form.

Merged `Stage14-4fg` writes the accepted normalized radial packet using one admissible squareclass divisor `L` and two reciprocal physical windows. Merged `Stage14-s7-89` gives the same coordinate through

```text
n=J1*a1*b1,
L=J1*a1^2,
```

with the complementary factor encoded reciprocally. `Stage14-Work-brX30` proves that these are the same global/s receiver up to already-frozen finite fibers, so their counts must not be multiplied.

The fixed-U route also has reciprocal hyperbola geometry,

```text
N(gamma)*ell <= X_U,
ell > 2*sqrt(B),
[gamma][a][pi_ell]=1,
```

but brX30 proves only a common geometric language, not a common arithmetic measure. The equality/existence-type divisor selector and the prime/projective-character inequality selector are therefore kept separate.

This is a material q-trigger: the formerly internal heavy-ray problem is now an exact classical-looking divisor-window object that q13 did not search.

## 2. Primary-source scan

### 2.1 Kevin Ford — distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval

Source:

- Kevin Ford, *The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval*, Annals of Mathematics 168 (2008), 367–433; arXiv `math/0401223`.

The source determines the order of magnitude of

```text
H(x,y,z)=#{n<=x : some d|n lies in (y,z]}
```

for all parameter ranges, and also studies the number of divisors in the interval.

Classification:

```text
FORD_2008_DIVISOR_INTERVAL=NEAR_GEOMETRY_HIGH_PRIORITY
DIRECT=false
```

Why it is close:

- the Stage14 global/s heavy receiver is now literally an existential divisor-window condition on one normalized radial integer;
- Ford's object is the canonical unrestricted model for exactly that geometric question;
- its parameter-uniform interval analysis is the correct first external architecture to test against the 4fg reciprocal windows.

Why it is not direct:

- Stage14 does not average over all integers `n<=x`; `n` comes from a charged physical packet with primitive/canonical/reverse-completion restrictions;
- the allowed divisor is not arbitrary: it carries the Stage14 squareclass form inherited from `L=J1*a1^2` and the complementary reciprocal root constraint;
- even an unrestricted divisor-window density estimate does not imply the same relative deficit inside an adversarially correlated physical subfamily;
- a logarithmic or expected-order density loss is not enough unless the active packet receives an actual `B^(-delta)` deficit.

Source:

- Kevin Ford, *Rough integers with a divisor in a given interval*, Journal of the Australian Mathematical Society 111 (2021), 17–36; arXiv `1901.02548`.

This determines, up to multiplicative constants and uniformly in the parameters, the number of integers with no small prime factor and a divisor in `(y,2y]`.

Classification:

```text

```

Why it matters:

Stage14 repeatedly freezes primitive/gcd/local decorations, so a rough-number model is closer than a completely unrestricted integer model if the remaining normalized radial support can be shown to inherit a genuine theorem-compatible roughness condition.

Missing hypotheses:

- no merged theorem identifies the physical radial measure with Ford's rough-integer ensemble;
- the admissible divisor still has the squareclass form and reciprocal partner window;
- the Stage14 window need not be exactly `(y,2y]` after packet normalization;
- the required relative saving must be measured against the Stage14 charged baseline, not against all rough integers.

### 2.3 Dimitris Koukoulopoulos — divisors of shifted primes

Source:

- Dimitris Koukoulopoulos, *Divisors of shifted primes*, International Mathematics Research Notices 2010, 4585–4627; arXiv `0905.0163`.

The paper gives lower bounds, in broad parameter ranges, for shifted primes `p+s` having a divisor in a prescribed interval, with the argument depending on distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions.

Classification:

```text

DIRECT=false
BRX30_ADAPTER_NO_GO_OVERRIDDEN=false
```

Why it is worth retaining:

It is a genuine primary-source example where a prime variable and a divisor-window condition are controlled in one theorem architecture, so it is the closest literature family found in this pass to a possible divisor-window/prime-window bridge.

Why it does not bridge brX30:

- fixed-U uses the hyperbolic product `N(gamma)*ell<=X_U`, not a shifted-prime value `p+s`;
- the selected prime is constrained by the projective relation `[gamma][a][pi_ell]=1`, not merely an ordinary arithmetic progression independent of the physical cofactor weight;
- tH29 already found no theorem-ready uniform treatment of the retained projective character sum with unrestricted endpoint headroom;
- no measure-preserving map from the global/s divisor-existence selector to this shifted-prime setting is supplied by the source.

Thus this paper is a bridge architecture to falsify quickly, not a license to cross-promote a saving.
