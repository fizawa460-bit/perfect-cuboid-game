# Stage14-q7 — Negative-results archive / anti-regression ledger

## Purpose

Stage14-q7 freezes the durable negative knowledge accumulated by the literature-radar route and the Stage14 proof tracks through the latest merged q6/s5l/t22 frontier.

The purpose is not to catalogue failed ideas for their own sake. It is to prevent future workers from repeatedly spending time on a tempting theorem, heuristic, or geometric mechanism whose mismatch is already understood.

Every entry has five fields:

- **STATUS** — `BLOCKED`, `CONSUMED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `DEFERRED`, or `DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY`;
- **FALSE INFERENCE PREVENTED** — the precise claim that must not be made;
- **WHY** — the mathematical incompatibility or later theorem that invalidates the shortcut;
- **REOPEN TRIGGER** — the exact new information required before revisiting the route;
- **OWNER / TARGET** — the active track to which the decision matters.

A route marked `BLOCKED` is not claimed impossible forever. It is blocked under the current formulation and may be reopened only by its stated trigger.

### Q7-A01 — arbitrary-matrix quadratic large sieve

**STATUS:** `BLOCKED`

**FALSE INFERENCE PREVENTED:** a quadratic large-sieve theorem for separated coefficients can be applied directly to the original correlated Euclid incidence matrix `W(u,v)`.

**WHY:** Heath-Brown/Liu and Wilson control sums with genuinely separated coefficient sequences. q2 found no theorem allowing an arbitrary two-variable coefficient matrix. Later s5i--s5k showed that the right move is not to search for such a theorem: the pure Euclid bulk separates after state/modulus freezing, while the linear discrepancy reduces to explicit projective/determinant incidence.

**REOPEN TRIGGER:** only if a future stage produces a genuinely new two-variable weight not reducible to the present state-frozen incidence and proves that it satisfies the precise hypotheses of a more general bilinear theorem.

**OWNER / TARGET:** `14-s`, `14-4`.

### Q7-C08 — t22 fixed-direction `B^o(1)` multiplicity closes the triple problem
