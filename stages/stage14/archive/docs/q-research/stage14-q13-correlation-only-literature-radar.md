## Family A — de la Bretèche--Tenenbaum multivariable polynomial mean values

Source: Régis de la Bretèche and Gérald Tenenbaum, *Mean values of arithmetic functions and application to sums of powers*, arXiv:2403.19320; Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 180 (2026), 1--13.

Classification: `NEAR_ARCHITECTURE_HIGH_PRIORITY_NO_POWER_DEFICIT_YET`.

The paper develops upper bounds for nonnegative arithmetic functions of several integer arguments evaluated on tuples of polynomial values. This is substantially closer to the new low-common-core object than q12: the Stage14 polynomial tuple can be taken schematically as

```text
Q1(a,b)=a,
Q2(a,b)=b,
Q3(a,b)=a^2+b^2,
```

while the canonical allocation witness is supported on simultaneous divisor data

```text
d_a | Q1,
d_b | Q2,
d_+ | Q3.
```

The source is therefore a plausible host for an **upper-envelope / multiplicity** estimate after the Stage14 canonical allocation weight is written as a nonnegative arithmetic function of the three polynomial values.

It is not DIRECT at the merged receiver. The Stage14 weight is not merely a product of three independent divisor functions: it imposes balanced windows, primitive/gcd restrictions, Gaussian split-prime conjugate allocation, a charged-once canonical witness convention, and inherited physical masks. No merged stage proves that this exact weight lies in the source's admissible multivariable class with constants uniform in the moving Stage14 packet. More importantly, the currently known one-factor/balanced-divisor ledgers are exponent-neutral, so a general expected-order upper bound or logarithmic loss would not by itself give the required `B^{-delta}` density deficit.

## Family B — Erdős--Hooley Delta moments / divisors in multiplicative windows

Sources:

- Régis de la Bretèche and Gérald Tenenbaum, *On moments of the Erdős--Hooley Delta-function*, arXiv:2512.05652 (2025);
- Régis de la Bretèche and Gérald Tenenbaum, *Note on the mean value of the Erdős--Hooley Delta-function*, arXiv:2309.03958.

Classification: `BACKGROUND_STRONG_MULTIPLICITY_CONTROL_NOT_JOINT_SCARCITY`.

Balanced Stage14 divisor witnesses live in multiplicative-size windows, so the Erdős--Hooley Delta function is the right classical statistic for controlling how many divisors an integer can place in a short multiplicative interval. The recent moment bounds can therefore police exceptional high-multiplicity balanced-divisor fibers.

This does **not** reopen a standalone balanced-divisor saving. Merged Stage14 already forbids charging generic balanced-divisor existence as an independent fixed-power deficit. Delta-function mean/moment estimates control the number of candidate divisors in one integer; they do not prove that the simultaneous canonical event on `a`, `b`, and `a^2+b^2` is power sparse, nor do they encode Gaussian conjugate allocation and reciprocal/physical completion.

## Family C — binary-form / principal-ideal arithmetic-function averages

Sources:

- R. de la Bretèche and T. D. Browning, *Sums of arithmetic functions over values of binary forms*, arXiv:math/0604119, Acta Arith. 125 (2006), 291--304;
- T. D. Browning and E. Sofos, *Averages of arithmetic functions over principal ideals*, arXiv:1706.04331.

Classification: `BACKGROUND_UPPER_ENVELOPE`.

These results establish robust upper bounds for nonnegative arithmetic functions on binary-form values, including ideal-valued analogues over number fields. They are useful for confirming that divisor/ideal multiplicities attached to `a^2+b^2` can often be kept at expected-order or subpower cost.

They do not directly count the Stage14 simultaneous three-divisor event on the **same primitive pair** `(a,b)`, and they do not retain the canonical balanced allocation coupling between rational divisors of `a,b` and Gaussian divisors of `a^2+b^2`. No fixed-power Stage14 deficit is imported.

## Family E — Wright 2026 partially fixed moduli / unbalanced convolution dispersion

Source: Thomas Wright, *Trilinear Kloosterman fractions I: partially fixed moduli and unbalanced convolutions*, arXiv:2604.25177 (2026).

Classification: `NEAR_CONDITIONAL_ON_MODULUS_AVERAGE_AND_SIEGEL_WALFISCH`.

Wright improves trilinear Kloosterman-fraction bounds when the denominator contains a fixed factor and derives stronger Linnik-dispersion estimates for unbalanced convolutions. The paper explicitly treats divisor-bounded coefficients but requires, in the distribution theorem, a small-modulus equidistribution / Siegel--Walfisz condition for one input sequence and an average over a polynomial family of moduli.

This makes it a poor direct fit to the **concentrated exact-`C`** branch, where one correlated modulus is frozen, but a plausible architecture for the **diffuse-`C`** branch if 4ep's variable-modulus Gaussian norm-factor correlation is further decomposed into a convolution whose candidate sequence satisfies the required small-modulus equidistribution.

## Family F — Pascadi 2024 exceptional-spectrum large sieve / dispersion

Source: Alexandru Pascadi, *Large sieve inequalities for exceptional Maass forms and the greatest prime factor of n^2+1*, arXiv:2404.04239 (2024).

Classification: `NEAR_ARCHITECTURE_AFTER_EXPLICIT_SPECTRAL_TRANSFORM`.

Pascadi proves large-sieve bounds for exceptional Maass-form coefficients weighted by sequences with sparse Fourier transforms and obtains improved multilinear Kloosterman-sum estimates used in dispersion problems. This is relevant if the Stage14 projective-collision or diffuse-modulus receiver reaches a Kuznetsov/Kloosterman spectral form with a sparse-transform coefficient.

No merged Stage14 receiver has yet produced that spectral object. The present candidate weights are physical/canonical-allocation weights, not a verified sparse-Fourier sequence in the sense required there. This source is therefore secondary to the direct Kloosterman-fraction transfer test above.

## Family G — Grimmelt--Merikoski 2025 divisor function in progressions / binary polynomial values

Source: Lasse Grimmelt and Jori Merikoski, *The divisor function along arithmetic progressions and binary cubic polynomials*, arXiv:2508.17979 (2025).

Classification: `NEAR_CONDITIONAL_STRUCTURED_MODULUS`.

The paper proves new distribution estimates for the divisor function in arithmetic progressions to moduli with two small factors and applies the technology to divisor averages along a binary cubic polynomial. This is relevant to q13 because Stage14's low-core weight is a divisor-allocation correlation and the diffuse branch contains a variable common-core modulus.

Direct use fails because the Stage14 modulus and divisor variables are generated from the same primitive pair and are coupled to `a^2+b^2`, while the published hypotheses require a specific arithmetic-progression/modulus factorization and do not include the full Gaussian/canonical physical selector. Reopen only if the internal route exposes an actual two-small-factor modulus decomposition with the candidate sequence independent enough for the stated distribution theorem.
