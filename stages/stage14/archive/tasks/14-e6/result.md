## 2. Peyre normalization

For the frozen e3/e4 surface

\[
Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{4}(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1),

\[

Here `H` is the actual physical adelic projective height: Euclidean at the real place and the usual max projective norm at finite places.

Thus e6 has three separate obligations:

## 3. Effective cone of the four-corner blowup

Use the integral Picard basis

\[
H_1,H_2,E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4,
\]

where `H1,H2` are the two rulings and the `Ej` are the four exceptional divisors.

The eight torus-invariant boundary divisors have classes

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4,\\[2mm]
H_1-E_1-E_2,&H_1-E_3-E_4,\\
H_2-E_1-E_3,&H_2-E_2-E_4.
\end{array}
\]

For a complete toric variety the effective cone is generated by invariant effective divisors, so these eight classes generate `Eff(Y)`.

Let a dual functional have coordinates

\[
(a,b,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4).
\]

Membership in the dual effective cone is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i&\ge0,\\
a&\ge c_1+c_2,\qquad a\ge c_3+c_4,\\
b&\ge c_1+c_3,\qquad b\ge c_2+c_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since

\[
-K_Y=2H_1+2H_2-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4,
\]

the Peyre slice is cut by

\[
2a+2b-(c_1+c_2+c_3+c_4)\le1.
\]

## 4. Exact effective-cone volume

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
S&=c_1+c_2+c_3+c_4,\\
r&=c_1+c_2-c_3-c_4,\\
k&=c_1-c_2+c_3-c_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The minimum permitted values of `a,b` are the larger row and column sums.  Therefore, after writing the remaining nonnegative slacks as `A,B`, the anticanonical functional becomes

\[
S+|r|+|k|+2A+2B.
\]

Define

\[
f(c)=S+|r|+|k|.
\]

For fixed `c` with `f(c)<=1`, integration over the two slack variables gives area

\[
\frac{(1-f(c))^2}{8}.
\]

The function `f` is homogeneous of degree one.  If

\[
K=\{c_i\ge0:f(c)\le1\},
\]

then radial integration in four dimensions gives

\[
\int_K(1-f)^2\,dc=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{15}.
\]

Hence the six-dimensional Peyre sublevel volume is

\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{120}.
\]

To compute `vol(K)`, add the fourth Hadamard coordinate

\[
\ell=c_1-c_2-c_3+c_4.
\]

The Jacobian from `(S,r,k,ell)` to `(c1,c2,c3,c4)` is `1/16`.  For fixed `S,r,k`, positivity of all four `ci` gives an `ell` interval of length

\[
2\bigl(S-\max(|r|,|k|)\bigr).
\]

Using the four sign quadrants and then the symmetry `R=|r| >= K=|k|`, the remaining integral is

\[
\operatorname{vol}(K)
=
\int_{R\ge K\ge0,\;2R+K\le1}
\frac12(1-2R-K)^2\,dK\,dR.
\]

Split at `R=1/3`:

\[
\int_0^{1/3}\int_0^R\frac12(1-2R-K)^2\,dK\,dR
=\frac{13}{1944},
\]

\[
\int_{1/3}^{1/2}\int_0^{1-2R}\frac12(1-2R-K)^2\,dK\,dR
=\frac1{3888}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{vol}(K)=\frac1{144}},
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{vol}
\{y\in\operatorname{Eff}(Y)^\vee:\langle y,-K_Y\rangle\le1\}
=
\frac1{17280}.
\]

Peyre's convention multiplies this sublevel volume by the Picard rank, hence

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(Y)=6\cdot\frac1{17280}=\frac1{2880}.
}
\]

## 8. Odd-prime local factor

Fix an odd prime `p` and let `n=v_p(q)`.

Because `2` is a unit, if `n!=0` then

\[
\left|\frac{q-q^{-1}}2\right|_p=p^{|n|},
\]

while for `n=0` one has `|t|_p<=1`.  Hence

\[
h_p(q):=\max(1,|t(q)|_p)=p^{|n|}.
\]

With additive Haar measure normalized by `vol(Z_p)=1`, every multiplicative valuation shell has

\[
\left|\frac{dq}{q}\right|\text{-measure}=1-\frac1p.
\]

Let `c=1-p^-1`.  In one variable the height level `0` has mass `c`, and every level `k>=1` has mass `2c`.  Therefore the cumulative mass through level `k` is

\[
c(2k+1).
\]

For two variables the mass for which the maximum level is exactly `k>=1` is

\[
c^2\bigl((2k+1)^2-(2k-1)^2\bigr)=8kc^2.
\]

Thus the unregularized local integral is

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_p
&=c^2\left(1+8\sum_{k\ge1}kp^{-k}\right)\\
&=c^2\left(1+\frac{8p^{-1}}{(1-p^{-1})^2}\right)\\
&=\boxed{1+\frac6p+\frac1{p^2}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The split Picard group has rank six, so the Peyre convergence factor is

\[
(1-p^{-1})^6.
\]

Therefore, for every odd prime,

\[
\boxed{
\tau_p=
\left(1-\frac1p\right)^6
\left(1+\frac6p+\frac1{p^2}\right).
}
\]

As a check, the toric integral model has

\[
\#Y(\mathbf F_p)=p^2+6p+1,
\]

so the parenthesis is exactly `#Y(F_p)/p^2` at every good odd prime.

## 9. The physical `p=2` factor

The prime `2` cannot be inserted into the odd-prime formula blindly.  The physical sections contain coefficients `2` and `4`, and the projective metric has bad reduction there.

Let `n=v_2(q)`.

For `n=0`, `q` is an odd 2-adic unit and

\[
v_2(q^2-1)\ge3,
\]

so

\[
v_2\left(\frac{q-q^{-1}}2\right)\ge2.
\]

Hence `h_2(q)=1` on the unit shell.

For `n!=0`, the two terms have unequal valuation, so

\[
\left|\frac{q-q^{-1}}2\right|_2=2^{|n|+1}.
\]

Each multiplicative valuation shell has `|dq/q|_2`-measure `1/2`.  Consequently the one-variable height-level masses are

and the two-variable integral before the global-frame factor is

\[

\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{2(k-1)}{2^k}

Now

\[

For comparison, substituting `p=2` into the good-prime point-count expression would give `17/256`; that is **not** the physical 2-adic metric used by Stage14-e.  The correction factor relative to that good-prime expression is `36/17`.
