## 1. Frozen object

On the canonical allocation-bearing primitive-slope family `Omega_can(B)`, with slope height `B^(1/4+o(1))`, every slope `omega` has only `B^o(1)` charged-once opposite-reciprocal candidates

```text
(C0,X0,Y0) in R(omega)
```

with

```text
gcd(X0,Y0)=1,
gcd(C0,X0*Y0)=1.
```

All odd prime factors of `C0` are already on the frozen Gaussian-split support, and the local square root of `-1` is part of the frozen orientation data. The live selector is

```text
C0 | X0^2+Y0^2,
```

or equivalently, after assembling the frozen root,

```text
X0 == i_C*Y0 (mod C0),
i_C^2 == -1 (mod C0).
```

The required result is a uniform conditional density bound

```text
# {omega in Omega_can(B): exists candidate satisfying the root line}
  << |Omega_can(B)| B^(-delta+o(1))
```

for some fixed `delta>0`, retaining the actual correlation between the canonical allocation, the primitive slope, `C0`, the candidate vector and all physical masks.

## 2. First obstruction: no fixed-power lower scale for C0 is available

For one split prime modulus `p==1 mod 4`, there are two roots of `-1` modulo `p`. Among unit pairs `(X,Y) mod p`, the root condition has density

```text
2/(p-1).
```

For squarefree split-supported `C0`, the corresponding model density is

```text
2^omega(C0)/phi(C0).
```

Thus a polynomially growing `C0` can in principle supply power-scale principal density loss, but a fixed or subpolynomial `C0` supplies only constant or `B^(-o(1))` density loss. The frozen s7-71 contract does not give a fixed positive lower exponent for `C0` on every canonical-allocation packet.

In particular the root-line shape itself cannot imply a uniform `B^-delta` bound. For `C0=5`, the primitive unit-pair root-line density is `1/2`.

```text
C0_FIXED_POWER_LOWER_BOUND_PROVED=false
ROOT_LINE_PRINCIPAL_DENSITY_UNIFORMLY_POWER_SPARSE=false
```

This is a theorem-applicability boundary, not a claim that every physical packet contains a positive proportion of `C0=5` candidates.

## 3. Second obstruction: the background is conditionally correlated with the modulus and vector

Existing equidistribution and large-sieve results for roots of quadratic congruences obtain cancellation or discrepancy control when roots/moduli are averaged against theorem-compatible smooth, interval, prime, spectral, or separated coefficient families.

Here the summation family is instead

```text
omega in Omega_can(B),
(C0,X0,Y0) in R(omega),
```

where `Omega_can(B)` is itself defined by simultaneous balanced integer/Gaussian allocation conditions and the candidate triple is reconstructed from the same witness. No merged stage proves that the induced sequence is equidistributed modulo `C0`, satisfies a Siegel-Walfisz hypothesis, factors into independent Type-I/Type-II coefficients, or has an acceptable spectral/Kloosterman transform.

Therefore an unweighted root-equidistribution theorem cannot be conditioned onto `Omega_can(B)` without a new adapter.

```text
CANONICAL_BACKGROUND_PSEUDORANDOMNESS_ADAPTER_PROVED=false
C0_CANDIDATE_INDEPENDENCE_ASSUMED=false
```

## 4. Duke-Friedlander-Iwaniec type root equidistribution

The classical DFI phenomenon and later refinements show equidistribution of roots of quadratic congruences under natural modulus averages; sectorial refinements for roots of `x^2+1` modulo primes also exist.

They are not direct adapters here because:

1. the physical `C0` may be composite and is generated by the same allocation witness as `(X0,Y0)`;
2. the target is a conditional density inside `Omega_can(B)`, not the unweighted root set;
3. all canonical allocation, dyadic/range/angular, squarefree/coprime and chart masks must remain;
4. no theorem-compatible weight decomposition of the conditional background is proved;
5. the theorem must be uniform over every frozen packet, including packets with no fixed-power lower scale for `C0`.

```text
DIRECT_GAUSSIAN_ROOT_EQUIDISTRIBUTION_THEOREM_APPLICABLE=false
```

## 5. Large sieve for quadratic roots

Large-sieve technology for roots of quadratic congruences is designed to exploit spacing/averaging across a family of roots and moduli. The frozen receiver supplies only the actual physical modulus associated with each correlated canonical-allocation candidate; it does not provide an independent polynomial modulus family with separated coefficients.

Moreover a large-sieve discrepancy bound would still have to be combined with the principal root-line density. On subpolynomial `C0` that principal density is not a fixed power.

```text
ROOT_LARGE_SIEVE_DIRECTLY_APPLICABLE=false

```

## 6. Bilinear modular-square-root / Salié dispersion

Modern bilinear modular-square-root and Salié-sum estimates give genuine power cancellation in specific prime-modulus and Type-I/Type-II regimes. They require explicit separated or bilinear coefficient geometry and quantitative range conditions.

No such decomposition is currently proved for the exact physical indicator

```text
1_{omega in Omega_can(B)}
1_{(C0,X0,Y0) in R(omega)}.
```

The `B^o(1)` candidate multiplicity does not create a separated polynomial variable, and divisor switching was already proved to be only a `B^o(1)` reparametrization.


## 8. Gaussian prime / Hecke technology

Gaussian prime and Hecke-character equidistribution can control prime orientation or class/sector averages. The s7-71 selector is not a prime-orientation question after localization: the root orientation is already frozen. What remains is incidence of the reconstructed candidate vector with that root line.

Consequently Gaussian-prime/Hecke cancellation does not remove the conditional principal root-line mass and is not a direct theorem for this receiver.
