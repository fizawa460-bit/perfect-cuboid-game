# Literature radar — 14-s

Current frontier: explain the first-small-point gate after Stage14-s3: physical hit `d<=B` forces a non-torsion elliptic point in a logarithmic canonical-height window, but no uniform least-generator/small-point distribution theorem has been imported.

## DIRECT / NEAR

### Petsche — Small rational points on elliptic curves over number fields
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0508160
- Theorem: Theorem 2
- Gives: for `E/k`, every non-torsion point satisfies `hhat(P) >= c(d,sigma) log |N Delta_min|`, with explicit polynomial loss in the field degree and Szpiro ratio.
- Stage14 hook: for `k=Q`, this is an explicit lower barrier that can be placed against the s3 upper window `O(log B+log H)`. The next step is concrete: compute/bound `Delta_min` and the conductor of `W^2=Z(Z-S^2)(Z+X^2)` along primitive Pythagorean `(S,X,H)`.
- Fit: NEAR (directly testable)
- Missing: the `sigma^{-6}`-type loss may make the bound too weak unless the Szpiro ratio stays uniformly controlled or grows very slowly on the Stage14 family.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

### Naccarato — Counting rational points on elliptic curves with a rational 2-torsion point
- Link: https://doi.org/10.4171/RLM/945
- Theorem: Proposition 4.4 is the Petsche lower-bound input used in the point-counting argument; Theorem 1.1 gives the resulting bounded-height rational-point estimate for curves with rational 2-torsion.
- Gives: a worked proof architecture that starts from a smallest-canonical-height lower bound in terms of minimal discriminant/Szpiro ratio and feeds it into lattice packing/counting.
- Stage14 hook: useful not because Stage14 needs another fixed-fiber multiplicity bound, but because it shows exactly what conductor/discriminant information must be supplied for a quantitative height barrier to survive.
- Fit: NEAR (proof-template value)
- Missing: Stage14 needs distribution of the *first physical* non-torsion point over moving Pythagorean bases, not only total rational-point counts on one fixed curve.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

### Le Boudec — Height of rational points on congruent number elliptic curves
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07136
- Theorem: Theorem 1; the proof interface starts with Lemma 1 and Lemma 2.
- Gives: a positive proportion of squarefree congruent-number twists have a strong lower bound on the canonical height of the lowest non-torsion rational point, proved by a large-prime-factor restriction plus complete 2-descent.
- Stage14 hook: this remains the closest structural analogue to the actual missing statistic in s3. The paper now gives a concrete two-lemma transplant target rather than only a general method analogy.
- Fit: NEAR (high-priority method transfer)
- Missing: fixed quadratic-twist family (`j` constant) versus Stage14's non-isotrivial Pythagorean family.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

#### Exact Le Boudec proof interface to test against Stage14-s1

- **Lemma 1 (large-prime-factor population):** restricts to squarefree parameters `d=mp` with `m <= X^theta`, `p` prime, `mp > X^(2 theta)`, and proves this subset still has cardinality `>> X`. The key uniqueness observation is that for `theta<1/2`, the large prime `p>X^theta` cannot divide the small cofactor of a second representation.
- **Lemma 2 (small-point suppression after descent):** bounds the number of parameters in the large-prime-factor population that admit a rational point below the target height. The proof explicitly invokes the earlier complete-2-descent parametrization and then counts the resulting divisor variables.
- Stage14 translation question A: can primitive Pythagorean bases `(S,X,H)` be restricted to a positive-density (or quantitatively large enough) subpopulation where one of the relevant split factors has a uniquely identifiable large prime divisor?
- Stage14 translation question B: after the s1 split covering equations, does that large prime force one descent variable into a constrained factor exactly as in Le Boudec, leaving a divisor-variable count with enough saving?
- Stage14 translation question C: is the resulting saving measured in the same base-height variable that controls physical `d<=B`, or does the Pythagorean parametrization destroy it?

This is now the preferred literature-driven attack on `14-s`: **do not try to import Le Boudec's theorem; reproduce the Lemma-1/Lemma-2 architecture in Stage14 coordinates and see exactly where it fails or succeeds.**

### Le Boudec — Height of rational points on quadratic twists of a given elliptic curve
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdv068
- Theorem: distribution conjecture and partial results for the canonical height of the lowest non-torsion rational point as the twist varies
- Gives: confirms that the exact Stage14 statistic — the lowest non-torsion point as the family parameter moves — is already a deep object even in a fixed quadratic-twist family.
- Stage14 hook: normalization/template for a Stage14 least-point statistic and a warning not to replace it with rank or total point multiplicity.
- Fit: NEAR
- Missing: fixed twist family rather than the Stage14 moving elliptic surface.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

### Griffin--Ono--Tsai — Heights of points on elliptic curves over Q
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/15605
- Theorem: effective canonical-height lower bounds via ideal-class pairings
- Gives: an alternate explicit lower-bound mechanism.
- Stage14 hook: secondary fallback if the Szpiro-ratio route is too weak on selected subfamilies.
- Fit: NEAR / secondary
- Missing: auxiliary discriminants/class-number terms must be uniformly controlled.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

### Loughran--Salgado — Rank jumps on elliptic surfaces and the Hilbert property
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01987
- Theorem: rank-jump non-thinness under geometric hypotheses
- Gives: qualitative abundance of rank-jump fibers.
- Stage14 hook: confirms that positive rank and small-first-point frequency are genuinely different questions.
- Fit: NEAR
- Missing: no physical-height count or least-generator control.
- Checked: 2026-08-09

### Wong — Heights and the Specialization Map for Families of Elliptic Curves over P^n
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3255
- Theorem: specialization-height control for fixed generic sections
- Gives: a family/base-height bridge for points arising from sections.
- Stage14 hook: useful only if a future geometric classification turns active points into specializations of finitely many sections/multisections.
- Fit: NEAR
- Missing: current rank-jump points are not known to come from a fixed generic non-torsion section.
- Checked: 2026-08-09
