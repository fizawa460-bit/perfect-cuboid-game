Thus, outside O(1) exceptional v-values, each relevant polynomial is
squarefree and non-square.
Weil's bound on each good v-slice gives O(sqrt(p)) cancellation, and the
exceptional slices contribute O(p).
Hence:
### Theorem 112-A — CompleteTwoVariableMixedCharacterBounds
For every good odd prime p,
```text
sum_{u,v in (F_p^*)^2} chi_p(E(u,v))
 =O(p^(3/2)),
```
```text
sum_{u,v in (F_p^*)^2} chi_p(H(u,v))
 =O(p^(3/2)),
```
and
```text
sum_{u,v in (F_p^*)^2} chi_p(E(u,v)H(u,v))
 =O(p^(3/2)).
```
The implied constants are absolute.
Multiplying by the free nonzero choices of b,f gives:
### Theorem 112-B — CompleteFourVariableMixedCharacterBounds
```text
sum_{a,b,c,f in (F_p^*)}
 chi_p(E4)
 =O(p^(7/2)),
```
```text
sum chi_p(H4)
 =O(p^(7/2)),
```
and
```text

 =O(p^(7/2)).
```
Thus the ambient four-variable family has square-root-quality saving
relative to its p^4 volume for all three nontrivial squareclass moments.

# SECTION113 — explicit Fourier transform and the elementary double-sieve exponent ceiling
## 2026-09-27
## Status: exact prime-modulus Fourier decomposition + quantitative negative result for elementary completion
Section112 left the simultaneous square-sieve route at the incomplete four-variable character sums.
The special ratio structure of E4 and H4 makes their additive Fourier transforms explicit.
## 113.1 Prime-modulus Fourier reduction
Let F(u,v) denote any one of E(u,v), H(u,v), or E(u,v)H(u,v).
For a good odd prime p define
S_p(alpha,beta,gamma,delta;F)
 = sum_{a,b,c,f in F_p^*}
   chi_p(F(a/b,c/f))
   e_p(alpha*a+beta*b+gamma*c+delta*f).
Substitute a=u*b and c=v*f. Then
S_p
 = sum_{u,v in F_p^*}
   chi_p(F(u,v))
   B_p(alpha*u+beta)
   B_p(gamma*v+delta),
where
B_p(L)=sum_{x in F_p^*} e_p(Lx)
      =-1+p*1_{L=0}.
Thus the full four-dimensional Fourier transform reduces exactly to the two-dimensional character sum plus at most two line restrictions and one point restriction.
## 113.2 Fourier hierarchy
Expanding
B_p(L1)B_p(L2)
 =1-p*1_{L1=0}-p*1_{L2=0}+p^2*1_{L1=L2=0},
and using the Section112 Weil bounds gives:
### Theorem 113-A — ExplicitPrimeFourierBounds
Uniformly in the additive frequency:
1. Zero frequency gives S_p=O(p^(7/2)).
2. If exactly one variable pair (alpha,beta) or (gamma,delta) vanishes identically, while the other pair produces a genuine affine restriction, then S_p=O(p^(5/2)).
3. For generic nonzero frequency, where both affine restrictions are proper, S_p=O(p^2).
The O(p^2) generic bound is genuine four-dimensional square-root cancellation.
The same hierarchy holds for F=E, H and EH away from the finite bad-prime set.
## 113.3 Incomplete rectangular box bound
Let I_a,I_b,I_c,I_f be intervals of lengths A,B1,C,F and put V=A*B1*C*F.
Fourier completion modulo p, together with the standard L1 bound for an interval Fourier transform, gives:
### Theorem 113-B — PrimeIncompleteBoxCharacterBound
For any of chi_p(E4), chi_p(H4), chi_p(E4H4),
|sum_box chi_p(...)|
 << V*p^(-1/2)
    +p^(1/2)*(A*B1+C*F)*(log p)^2
    +p^2*(log p)^4.
The first term is the zero Fourier mode; the middle terms are the one-pair-degenerate modes; the last term is the generic dual contribution.
## 113.4 Squarefree composite moduli
For squarefree q supported on good primes, CRT multiplicativity gives, for every epsilon>0,
### Theorem 113-C — SquarefreeIncompleteBoxCharacterBound
|sum_box chi_q(...)|
 << q^epsilon * [
      V*q^(-1/2)
      +q^(1/2)*(A*B1+C*F)
      +q^2
    ].
The theorem applies separately to E4, H4 and to mixed characters obtained from disjoint squarefree moduli by CRT.
Primitive/coprime masks can be inserted later by Mobius inversion; they do not improve the exponent ceiling below.
## 113.5 Balanced-box test
Consider the generic balanced physical box
A~B1~C~F~R,
g~1.
Then V~R^4 and B_phys~R^4.
A standard square sieve with primes of size P creates correlation moduli q~P^2.
Theorem113-C gives the characteristic correlation scale
R^4/P + R^2*P + P^4.
The square-sieve diagonal term is also of size R^4/P.
Balancing R^4/P with P^4 gives
P~R^(4/5),
and therefore
### Theorem 113-D — ElementarySingleSquareCeiling
best elementary-completion scale ~ R^(16/5).
The middle term is then only R^(14/5).
## 113.6 Simultaneous two-square sieve ceiling
A symmetric two-square sieve for E4 square and H4 square uses two prime sets of comparable size P.
The fully off-diagonal terms have composite modulus q~P^4.
The generic incomplete Fourier term then contains q^2~P^8.
The double-diagonal main term is R^4/P^2.
Balancing
R^4/P^2 = P^8
gives
P~R^(2/5),
and endpoint scale
R^(16/5).
Partially diagonal terms do not improve this exponent.
### Negative Result 113-E — ElementaryDoubleSquareSieveCeiling
Even with the exact generic Fourier bound O(q^2), a standard two-stage square sieve plus elementary completion cannot beat
R^(16/5+epsilon)
in a balanced generic box.
Since B_phys~R^4, this is
B_phys^(4/5+epsilon).
The required absolute endpoint bound for the current denominator strategy is o(B_phys^(1/3)).
Therefore elementary completion misses the target by a very large exponent gap.
## 113.7 Interpretation
The failure is not caused by weak local complete sums.
Generic additive Fourier modes already attain the natural four-dimensional square-root scale O(q^2).
The obstruction is global: the sieve modulus needed to force integer squares must grow much faster than the side lengths of the physical parameter box.
Thus merely improving complete finite-field character sums cannot close the problem.
A successful ambient double-square method would require an additional structural mechanism such as:
1. q-van der Corput differencing lowering the effective dual dimension;
2. determinant/spacing information from gcd strata;
3. a low-dimensional exceptional decomposition with much smaller effective volume;
4. a relative argument against M3(B), rather than an absolute endpoint count.
## 113.8 Consequence for Bonolis–Browning style methods
Bonolis–Browning obtain extra saving beyond elementary completion by q-analogue van der Corput differencing and a nontrivial oscillatory-sum estimate.
For the present family, such technology would be necessary even to improve substantially on B^(4/5).
However the final B^(1/3) target is far beyond the gain produced by a single mild differencing step in analogous hyperelliptic-fibration problems.
Thus the ambient double-square route remains useful as a secondary route and as a source of quantitative upper bounds, but should not presently replace the structural one-factor/norm-core route as the sole strategy.
## 113.9 Revised priority
Structural primary:
return to the primitive norm/common-core architecture and seek a relative-to-M3 power saving, where only o(M3(B)) is required.
Analytic secondary:
develop q-van der Corput for the four-variable E4/H4 family only if it yields an explicit exponent materially below 4/5.
