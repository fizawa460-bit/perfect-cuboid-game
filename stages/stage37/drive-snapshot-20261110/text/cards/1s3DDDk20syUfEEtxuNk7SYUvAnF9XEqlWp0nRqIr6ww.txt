2. Canonical four-atom decomposition of the three pair radicals


Define
  chi := gcd(r_e,r_y,r_x),                            (229BW.4)
and
  u_U := gcd(r_e,r_x)/chi,
  u_V := gcd(r_e,r_y)/chi,
  u_z := gcd(r_y,r_x)/chi.                           (229BW.5)


All four quantities are squarefree positive integers. Prime-by-prime:
- chi consists exactly of primes dividing at least two of g_U,g_V,g_z;
- u_U consists exactly of primes dividing g_U and neither g_V nor g_z;
- u_V consists exactly of primes dividing g_V and neither g_U nor g_z;
- u_z consists exactly of primes dividing g_z and neither g_U nor g_V.


Hence
  gcd(chi,u_U u_V u_z)=1,
  gcd(u_U,u_V)=gcd(u_V,u_z)=gcd(u_z,u_U)=1.          (229BW.6)


The three pair radicals reconstruct exactly as
  r_e = chi u_U u_V,
  r_y = chi u_V u_z,
  r_x = chi u_z u_U.                                (229BW.7)


Also

and therefore

    = chi^3 u_U^2 u_V^2 u_z^2
    = chi [rad(g_U g_V g_z)]^2.                     (229BW.9)


Equivalently,

is an exact integer square.

3. Physical localization of odd pair overlaps


For a positive integer n write rad_odd(n) for the product of its odd prime divisors.


Let p be odd.


If p|g_U and p|g_V, then p|R-U and p|R-V, so U≡V≡R mod p. Using
  R^2-U^2=y^2,
  R^2-V^2=x^2,
we get p|x and p|y. Therefore
  p|d0=gcd(x,y).                                    (229BW.10)


If p|g_U and p|g_z, then U≡z≡R mod p. From
  R^2-U^2=y^2,
  R^2-z^2=e^2,
we obtain p|e,y and therefore
  p|a=gcd(e,y).                                     (229BW.11)


If p|g_V and p|g_z, similarly
  p|b=gcd(e,x).                                     (229BW.12)


Define the three odd overlap atoms

  chi_Uz := rad_odd(gcd(g_U,g_z)),
  chi_Vz := rad_odd(gcd(g_V,g_z)).                 (229BW.13)


Then exactly

  chi_Uz | rad(a),

Thus the odd part of the common pair-radical overlap is

4. Split-only content buckets lie in three distinct unique atoms


Retain the pairwise-content property of integral Pythagorean triples:
- (U,y,R) has content c12, so gcd(U,R)=gcd(y,R)=c12;
- (V,x,R) has content c21, so gcd(V,R)=gcd(x,R)=c21;
- (e,z,R) has content h, so gcd(z,R)=gcd(e,R)=h.    (229BW.17)


Let p be an odd prime dividing c12. Then p|R and p|U, so p|g_U.
Pairwise coprimality gives p∤c21 and p∤h. Hence p∤V and p∤z by (229BW.17), so p∤g_V and p∤g_z.
Therefore p belongs to the unique g_U atom u_U.


Thus
  rad(c12) | u_U.                                  (229BW.18)


Similarly,

  rad(h)   | u_z.                                  (229BW.20)


Here h,c12,c21 are odd, so no 2-adic qualification is needed for these inclusions.
