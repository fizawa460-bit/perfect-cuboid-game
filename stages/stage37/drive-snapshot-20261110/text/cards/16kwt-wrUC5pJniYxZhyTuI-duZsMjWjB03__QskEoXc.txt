are BOTH non-torsion points of E_d(Q), where

2. Exact Gaussian dependence normal form


Assume A,C are dependent modulo torsion. Work in the free lattice
  E_d(Q)/E_d(Q)_tors.
Let the saturated rank-one subgroup containing A,C be generated by the class of a non-torsion R in E_d(Q).


Then there are nonzero coprime integers a,b and rational torsion points T_A,T_C such that
  A=aR+T_A,
  C=bR+T_C,
  gcd(a,b)=1,
  ab!=0.                                        (229S.2)


The nonzero assertion uses 229R: neither A nor C is torsion.


For squarefree d>1, retain the 229Q torsion firewall
  E_d(Q(i))_tors=E_d[2].
Using 229Q/229R
  2P=A-iota(C),
we obtain exactly
  2P=[a-ib]R+T,                                 (229S.3)
where
  T=T_A-iota(T_C) in E_d[2].


Thus every dependent physical point lies on a Gaussian multiplication/division carrier with primitive Gaussian slope
  alpha=a-ib in Z[i],
  N(alpha)=a^2+b^2.

3. Exact height identity for the dependence coefficients


Torsion has canonical height zero, so from (229S.2)

Combining with (229S.1),
  (a^2+b^2) hhat(R)=4 hhat(P).                  (229S.4)


This is exact. In particular the Gaussian norm is not an auxiliary parameter: it is exactly the ratio
  N(alpha)=4 hhat(P)/hhat(R).                   (229S.5)

4. Uniform lower height on the quadratic-twist family


Use the standard fixed-base quadratic-twist canonical-height lower bound recorded in Nara, Remark 1.2, citing Silverman AEC Exercise 8.16(c).


Nara records, in his normalization,
  hhat_N(R) > (1/4) log d + O_E(1)
for non-torsion rational R on a quadratic twist by squarefree d, and explicitly notes that his canonical height is twice the Silverman-standard normalization.


Therefore in the standard Stage37 normalization there is a fixed constant C_E such that
  hhat(R) >= (1/8) log d - C_E                 (229S.6)
for every non-torsion R in E_d(Q).


This use is the general quadratic-twist bound from Nara Remark 1.2; it does NOT invoke Nara Theorem 1.1 or its sixth-power-free discriminant hypothesis.

6. Exact exclusion of the smallest possible slope norm


Because a,b are both nonzero and coprime, the smallest possible Gaussian norm is
  a^2+b^2=2,
which forces
  |a|=|b|=1.
Then (229S.2) gives
  C=+A+T
or
  C=-A+T
for some rational T in E[2].                    (229S.12)


We prove that both possibilities are physically impossible.


Write
  z=p^2=X(P)/d,
  w=conj(z)=X(sigma P)/d.
Retain from 229J/L that p has positive nonzero real and imaginary parts, and
  N(p), N(p^2+1), N(p^2-1)
are rational squares.


Translation by the three nonzero rational 2-torsion points
  T0=(0,0), T+=(d,0), T-=(-d,0)
acts on normalized x-coordinate u=X/d as
  u,
  -1/u,
  (u+1)/(u-1),
  (1-u)/(1+u).                                  (229S.13)


If C=+A+T, then
  [1+i](iP-sigma P)=T.
If C=-A+T, then
  [1+i](P-i sigma P)=T.
In either case the point inside [1+i](...) is torsion because its image is torsion under an isogeny. By the retained 229Q K-torsion firewall it lies in E[2]. Therefore in BOTH signs
  sigma P = +/- iP + Q
for some Q in E[2].


Since x(+/- iP)=-x(P), (229S.13) gives exactly four possibilities:
  w=-z,
  w=1/z,
  w=(z-1)/(z+1),
  w=(1+z)/(1-z).                                (229S.14)


Each is impossible:


(i) w=-z.
Since w=conj(z), z is purely imaginary. With
  p=lambda(x+i y), lambda>0
on the physical chart, Re(p^2)=0 forces x=y.
But the retained missing-face square has
  z_face^2=x^2+y^2=2x^2,
impossible for positive integers x,z_face.


(ii) w=1/z.
Then
  zw=1.
But
  zw=(p conj(p))^2=N(p)^2=v^4,
with
  v=(R_phys+e)/z_face>1
because R_phys^2=e^2+z_face^2 and e>0. Contradiction.


(iii) w=(z-1)/(z+1).
Then
  N(p^2+1)=(z+1)(w+1)=2z.
The left side is rational, so z is rational, contradicting that p^2 is non-real.


(iv) w=(1+z)/(1-z).
Then
  N(p^2-1)=(z-1)(w-1)=-2z.
Again z would be rational, contradiction.


Hence
  N(alpha)=2
is EMPTY on the positive physical population.   (229S.15)


Since norms 1,3,4 cannot occur with coprime nonzero a,b, any dependent physical point satisfies
  N(alpha)=a^2+b^2 >=5.                         (229S.16)

7. What changed and what remains open


Changed:
- 229R's abstract CM_PAIR_DEPENDENCE_GATE is now a finite bounded-slope problem on the current large radical tail;
- every dependent endpoint has exact normal form
    2P=[alpha]R+T
  with alpha in a B-independent finite set;
- the minimal norm-2 slope is eliminated exactly by the physical selector/missing-face conditions;
- any remaining dependence slope has Gaussian norm at least 5.


Still open:
- slopes of norms 5,10,13,... up to the finite bound M_delta are not eliminated;
- no theorem currently counts the physical points satisfying
    2P-T in [alpha]E_d(Q)
  with the required o(B^(1/3)) total;
- finite-degree carrier reduction alone is not a saving;
- A,C independence is NOT proved globally;
- no rank>=2 route is reopened.


The next high-value dependence attack is no longer an infinite slope problem. It is:


  BOUNDED_CM_IMAGE_GATE:
  for each primitive Gaussian alpha with
    5<=N(alpha)<=M_delta
  and each T in E[2],
  count or eliminate physical endpoints on the exact same measure satisfying
    2P-T in [alpha]E_d(Q).


A uniform elimination, or an o(B^(1/3)) total over this finite set, would close CM_PAIR_DEPENDENCE_GATE and permit a legitimate reassessment of independent-pair/rank>=2 inputs.


Do not infer sparsity merely from finite degree, and do not issue M3-T11-230 from this reduction alone.
