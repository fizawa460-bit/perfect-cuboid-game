2. Primitive points in an arbitrary planar lattice: no perimeter term
Lemma 229BF-PL.
Let Lambda be any full-rank sublattice of Z² of determinant/index M>=1, and Q>=0. Then
  #{v=(r,s) in Lambda : gcd(r,s)=1, r²+s²<=Q}
      << 1+Q/M                                                (229BF.1)
with an ABSOLUTE implied constant, uniformly in Lambda.
Proof. Write L=sqrt(Q). If Lambda has no nonzero point of length <=L, the count is zero. Otherwise let v be a shortest nonzero lattice vector, length lambda<=L. Because v is primitive INSIDE Lambda, extend it to a Z-basis (v,w) of Lambda. Every lattice point has a unique representation a*v+b*w. The determinant relation and the norm bound give
  |b|*M=|det(v,a*v+b*w)|<=lambda*L,
so |b|<=lambda*L/M.
For b=0, at most two vectors a*v can be primitive in Z²: if v itself is ambient-primitive, only a=+1 or -1 qualify; otherwise none. For each nonzero b, the possible points lie on a line spaced by lambda; the disk has chord length <=2L, hence at most 1+2L/lambda values of a. Therefore
  N <= 2+2*(lambda*L/M)*(1+2L/lambda)
    <= 2+2L²/M+4L²/M
    << 1+Q/M,
since lambda<=L. This proves the lemma. Importantly a generic lattice-point count has a perimeter term O(L/lambda); ambient primitivity kills the long sequence of multiples along the shortest lattice vector.
