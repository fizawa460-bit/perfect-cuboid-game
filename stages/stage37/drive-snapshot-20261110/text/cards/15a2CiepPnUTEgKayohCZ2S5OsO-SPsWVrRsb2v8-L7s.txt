2. Exact odd pair-gcd square theorem
Define
q_UV=sqrt(odd(gcd(A,Bv))),
q_Uz=sqrt(odd(gcd(A,C))),
q_Vz=sqrt(odd(gcd(Bv,C))).
These square roots are integers, not merely formal radicals.


Proof for q_UV:
An odd prime p dividing A and Bv divides y and x because
A(R+U)=y² and Bv(R+V)=x².
Such p cannot divide R: otherwise e²=R²-x²-y² implies p|e, contradicting primitive edges. Hence R,U,V are units modulo p and R+U,R+V are units modulo p. Consequently
v_p(A)=2v_p(y), v_p(Bv)=2v_p(x),
v_p(gcd(A,Bv))=2v_p(d0).
This proves that the odd gcd is square and that q_UV is the full prime-power subproduct of odd(d0) on precisely those primes for which R≡U≡V mod p. In particular q_UV|d0 and gcd(q_UV,R)=1.
The other two cases are identical:
at p|gcd(A,C), v_p(A)=2v_p(y), v_p(C)=2v_p(e), giving q_Uz|a;
at p|gcd(Bv,C), v_p(Bv)=2v_p(x), v_p(C)=2v_p(e), giving q_Vz|b.
By229BW/229BI, these three odd conductors are pairwise coprime. They need not be squarefree. They select full valuations from d0,a,b; they do not equal the whole buckets in general.
