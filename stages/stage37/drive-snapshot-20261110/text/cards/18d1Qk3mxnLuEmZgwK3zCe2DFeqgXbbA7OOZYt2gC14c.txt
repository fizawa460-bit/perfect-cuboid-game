N_m(B) <<_epsilon B^(1+epsilon)/m, for 1<=m<=sqrt(2B).

Proof, height cutoff. Each such (e,x,U) has gcd(e,x)=g with p not dividing g. The primitive triple (E,X,H)=(e/g,x/g,U/g) has m|E,p not dividing X, and H<=B/g. From (H-X)(H+X)=E², and p odd with p not dividing X, precisely one of H-X,H+X is divisible by p, and since m|E its p-valuation is at least 2n. Hence m²<=H+X<2H<=2B/g and so g<=2B/m². In particular m²<2B for positive E,X, giving the stated empty range (the weak boundary m²=2B can be retained harmlessly).


Proof, primitive triples. For fixed Hmax=T, every primitive Pythagorean triple (E,X,H) has Euclid parameters u>v>0, gcd(u,v)=1, opposite parity, H=u²+v²<=T, with E either 2uv or u²-v². Thus all possible (u,v) lie on at most FOUR residue lines u≡0, v≡0, u≡v or u≡-v (mod m). For every v<=sqrt(T), each line has <=sqrt(T)/m+1 allowable u<=sqrt(T). The total number of primitive triples with m|E, H<=T is O(T/m+sqrt(T)); excluding parity/coprimality only enlarges this upper count.


Proof, global scaling and completion. Sum the preceding estimate over 1<=g<=min(B,2B/m²), p not dividing g:
