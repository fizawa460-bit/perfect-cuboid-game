1. New external theorem species
A previously unregistered current theorem species was identified:
D. Bonolis, L. B. Pierce, K. Woo, “Counting integral points in thin sets of type II: singularities, sieves, and stratification”, arXiv:2505.11226 (2025).


For an absolutely irreducible F(Y,X_1,...,X_n) monic in Y and polynomial in Y^m, m>=2, their affine type-II thin-set theorem gives, under a genuine-variable/nondegeneracy hypothesis,
    N(F,T) << T^(n-1+1/(n+1)+epsilon).
For n=4 this exponent is
    3+1/5 = 16/5.
For a special class they obtain the stronger scale
    N(F,T) << T^(n-1) (log T)^e,
so for n=4 the optimistic strong scale is T^3 polylog.


The 2026 survey by the same authors, arXiv:2603.23334, emphasizes that coefficient-independent uniformity for affine type-II thin sets is still not known in general. The companion Bonolis–Kowalski–Woo stratification work, arXiv:2506.18299, provides uniform stratification in families but does not by itself turn the type-II count into the exact Stage37 moving-height measure.

3. Quantitative ceiling on a favorable regular balanced cell
Consider a regular balanced dyadic subcell of one chart in which all four raw Pythagorean parameters have scale T, the relevant O/E legs have scale T^2, and g is bounded. On such a subcell,
    H_theta has scale T^8,
so the exact physical cutoff R<=B has the favorable scale
    T <= B^(1/4)
up to fixed constants.


Grant, optimistically, every polynomial admissibility hypothesis needed by the 2025 theorem.


The general n=4 type-II bound then gives only
    T^(16/5+epsilon)
      = B^(4/5+epsilon)
on the favorable balanced physical scale.


Even granting the stronger special-class bound gives only
    T^3 polylog
      = B^(3/4) polylog.


The current certified Stage37 denominator corridor contains only
    M3(B) >> B^(1/3)
at the lower end.
Therefore neither B^(4/5+epsilon) nor the optimistic B^(3/4) polylog can by itself prove
    P(B)=o(M3(B)).


This is a method-sufficiency statement, not a lower bound on P. It says the cited black-box thin-set theorem does not have enough exponent budget to close the direct endpoint count from the currently certified M3 lower bound.


Notably the generic B^(4/5) physical exponent is the same exponent scale already appearing in the retained M3-FOUR-NEG-001 balanced elementary double-square-sieve ceiling. The new theorem is conceptually stronger and much more general, but its black-box n=4 exponent does not cross the Stage37 absolute endpoint threshold.

5. A/R1-TF centered-covariance test
The A route does not merely need an unsigned upper bound for one square-value set. It needs the signed same-measure covariance
    Z_j = sum_{H_j} (F-alpha)(S-beta)
with empirical global marginals and Pearson saving stronger than B^(-1/12).


The type-II theorem counts parameter points admitting an integral lift for one finite cover. It does not provide:
- an exact centered expansion for the two integer-square selectors;
- a principal term equal to the empirical product alpha*beta on the physical H_j measure;
- a signed covariance estimate;
- a moving-gcd physical coefficient theorem at the required Pearson normalization.


Encoding both E_theta and H_theta as one finite fiber-product cover does not by itself create a second independent thinness saving from the cited theorem; the theorem remains a type-II projected-point upper bound, not a centered two-selector correlation theorem.


Therefore
