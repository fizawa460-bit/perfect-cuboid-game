4. Gaussian coprimality separates every conjugate support


Because gcd(a,b)=1 and n is odd,
  gcd_{Z[i]}(alpha,bar(alpha))=1.                  (229AA.6)


Proof: a common Gaussian prime divides
  alpha+bar(alpha)=2a
and
  alpha-bar(alpha)=2bi.
The prime 1+i cannot divide alpha because n is odd. Any other common prime lies over an odd rational prime dividing both a and b, contradicting gcd(a,b)=1.


Hence choose u,v in Z[i] with
  u alpha+v bar(alpha)=1.                         (229AA.7)


Now compare the supports of A_alpha,B_alpha and their coefficient conjugates.


If a common X-root of A_alpha and bar(A_alpha) existed, choose P above that X-value. Since T0=-T0,
  [alpha]P=T0,
  [bar(alpha)]P=T0.
Equation (229AA.7) gives
  P=[u+v]T0 in E_d[2].
But [alpha] is bijective on E_d[2] and only T0 maps to T0, so P=T0, which is precisely the canceled point and is not a root of A_alpha. Contradiction.


If a common root of B_alpha and bar(B_alpha) existed, then
  [alpha]P=O,
  [bar(alpha)]P=O,
and (229AA.7) gives P=O, again the canceled point rather than a finite B_alpha-root.


If a common root occurred between a zero-support factor and a pole-support conjugate, for example
  [alpha]P=T0,
  [bar(alpha)]P=O,
then (229AA.7) gives P=[u]T0 in {O,T0}; neither possibility satisfies both equations. The other cross cases are identical.


Therefore all four finite support sets attached to
  A_alpha, bar(A_alpha), B_alpha, bar(B_alpha)
are pairwise disjoint except for the already canceled O/T0 points, and each is simple.
