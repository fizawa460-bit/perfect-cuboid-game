2. Coprime pullback through [1-i]


Fix the representative
  beta=2-i,    N(beta)=5.
All primitive norm-ten slopes are obtained from the corresponding alpha=(1+i)beta by units and conjugation; the physical norm-square packet is invariant under those finite operations up to swapping the + and - selectors.


Retain 229V:
  [1-i]P=[beta]R'.                                  (229X.1)


Because beta and 1-i are coprime in Z[i], choose A,B in Z[i] with
  A*beta+B*(1-i)=1.
Define
  Q=[A]P+[B]R'.
Then, using (229X.1),
  [1-i]Q
   =[A][1-i]P+[B][1-i]R'
   =[A beta+B(1-i)]R'
   =R'.                                             (229X.2)


Moreover
  [1-i](P-[beta]Q)=0,
so
  P=[beta]Q+S,    S in ker[1-i]={O,T0}, T0=(0,0).   (229X.3)


The T0 translate is harmless for the physical square conditions: on normalized x it sends z to -1/z. Since -1=i^2 is a K-square, z is a K-square iff -1/z is; and N of a Gaussian square root is inverted, so being a rational square is preserved. Likewise N(z+1) and N(z-1) are swapped up to division by N(z), preserving their square status on the physical packet.


Thus it suffices to analyze S=O.
