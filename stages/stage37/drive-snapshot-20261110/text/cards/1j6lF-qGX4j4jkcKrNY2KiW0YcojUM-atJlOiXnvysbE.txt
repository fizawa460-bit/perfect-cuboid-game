2. Exact 2-adic content trichotomy


Every primitive Pythagorean triple has exactly one even leg, and its even leg is divisible by 4.


Because gcd(g,d)=1, g and d cannot both be even. Applying the primitive parity rule simultaneously to T1,T2,T3 gives exactly three possible content patterns.


TYPE G-EVEN:
    g even, hence d odd.
    T1,T2 force c,f odd.
    T3 then forces a,b to have opposite parity.
    Since ga and gb are both even legs in T1,T2, v2(g)>=2.
    The even member of {a,b} is the even leg-factor in T3 and is itself divisible by 4.
Therefore
    4|g,
    d,c,f odd,
    a,b opposite parity,
    4 divides the even member of {a,b}.            (229E.6)


TYPE D-EVEN:
    d even, hence g odd.
    T1,T2 force a,b odd.
    T3 forces c,f to have opposite parity.
    Since dc and df are both even legs in T1,T2 and one of c,f is odd, v2(d)>=2.
    The even member of {c,f} is the even leg-factor in T3 and is divisible by 4.
Therefore
    4|d,
    g,a,b odd,
    c,f opposite parity,
    4 divides the even member of {c,f}.            (229E.7)


TYPE BOTH-ODD:
    g,d odd.
    T1 gives a,c opposite parity.
    T2 gives b,f opposite parity.
    gcd(a,b)=gcd(c,f)=1 excludes a,b both even and, together with the previous two relations, excludes a,b both odd.
Hence a,b are opposite parity and c,f are opposite parity.
Moreover the even factors occur crosswise:
    either a,f are even and b,c odd,
    or b,c are even and a,f odd.
Because g,d are odd, each even member is itself an even leg of a primitive face triple, hence each is divisible by 4.
Therefore the even leg of T3 is divisible by 16.             (229E.8)


These three cases are exhaustive and disjoint.


Since q is the hypotenuse of primitive T3, q is odd and q==1 mod4.
Hence
    h=gcd(g,q)
is always odd.
Every prime divisor of h is 1 mod4 by 229D, so in fact
    h==1 mod4.                                    (229E.9)
