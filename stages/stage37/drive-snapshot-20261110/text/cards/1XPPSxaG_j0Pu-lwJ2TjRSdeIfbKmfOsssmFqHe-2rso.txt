2. Exact CM pair Gram determinant


Work on
  E_d: Y^2=X^3-d^2X,
  K=Q(i),
with complex conjugation sigma and CM automorphism
  iota=[i],  iota(X,Y)=(-X,iY).


Use the symmetric Neron-Tate bilinear pairing
  <Q1,Q2>
with
  <Q,Q>=hhat(Q).


Put
  H=hhat(P),
  T=<P,sigma(P)>,
  U=<P,iota sigma(P)>.


Retain229R:
  A=P+sigma(P) in E_d(Q),
  C=iota(P-sigma(P)) in E_d(Q),
  hhat(A)+hhat(C)=4H.                            (229AC.1)


Under the principal polarization the Rosati adjoint of iota=[i] is
  iota^dagger=[-i].
Hence
  <Q,iota Q>=0                                   (229AC.2)
for every Q, because symmetry and the adjoint identity give
  <Q,iota Q>=<iota^dagger Q,Q>=<-iota Q,Q>=-<Q,iota Q>.


Therefore
  hhat(A)=2H+2T,
  hhat(C)=2H-2T.                                 (229AC.3)


For the cross term,
  <A,C>
   =<P+sigma P, iota P-iota sigma P>
   =-2U.                                         (229AC.4)


Thus the exact Gram determinant of the rational CM pair is


  Delta_CM(P)
   := det [[hhat(A), <A,C>],
           [<A,C>, hhat(C)]]
    =4(H^2-T^2-U^2).                             (229AC.5)


RESULT_229AC_CM_PAIR_GRAM_DETERMINANT=PROVED_EXACT.


Because the Neron-Tate form is positive definite on E_d(Q)/torsion,
  Delta_CM(P)=0
iff A,C are dependent modulo torsion,
and
  Delta_CM(P)>0
iff A,C are independent modulo torsion.          (229AC.6)


Combined with229AB, the Delta_CM(P)=0 physical locus on the preferred large-radical tail is B^{o(1)}.

4. Smith 2-infinity-Selmer theorem applies to the base twist family


The fixed base curve
  E: y^2=x^3-x
has full rational 2-torsion.


It also has no rational cyclic subgroup of order4. Indeed the duplication formula is
  x(2Q)=(x(Q)^2+1)^2/[4x(Q)(x(Q)^2-1)].
Halving the three rational 2-torsion points would require respectively
  x(Q)=+/-i,
  x(Q)=1+/-sqrt(2),
  x(Q)=-1+/-sqrt(2),
so no order-four cyclic subgroup is Galois-stable over Q.


The quadratic twist model
  d y^2=x^3-x
is Q-isomorphic to the Stage37 congruent-number model
  Y^2=X^3-d^2X
via
  X=d x,  Y=d^2 y.                               (229AC.8)


Therefore Alexander Smith's theorem species applies: among squarefree twists |d|<X, the number with Mordell-Weil rank at least2 is o(X).


RESULT_229AC_SMITH_RANK2_THEOREM_SPECIES=APPLICABLE_TO_BASE_TWIST_FAMILY.


But this is quantitatively insufficient for Stage37. Retain
  d<=2B^2
and the uniform fixed-d endpoint fiber B^{o(1)} from229L.
An o(X) rank-two support theorem at X asymptotic to B^2 yields only
  o(B^2)*B^{o(1)},
which does NOT imply o(B^{1/3}).


RESULT_229AC_SMITH_oX_DIRECT_STAGE37_MATCH=NO_GO_AT_CURRENT_TWIST_SCALE.

6. Two-Selmer distribution is not the missing count


Modern distribution theorems for full-rational-2-torsion twist families determine positive-density distributions of 2-Selmer ranks.


But
  rank E_d(Q) <= dim Sel_2(E_d)-dim E_d(Q)[2],
so using a high 2-Selmer-rank condition as an upper envelope for rank>=2 leaves positive-density Selmer strata. Such information alone supplies no X^{gamma} upper bound with gamma<1, much less the required gamma<1/6.


Smith's 2-infinity-Selmer theorem is stronger because it gives actual rank>=2 support o(X), but Section4 shows even that is far too weak at X~B^2.


RESULT_229AC_TWO_SELMER_DISTRIBUTION_ALONE=NO_GO_FOR_ONE_THIRD_BUDGET.


7. Petit determinant-method two-small-point route: contract mismatch


Joachim Petit's second-moment method is much closer in shape to the Stage37 independent-pair problem: it controls off-diagonal pairs of small rational points on the same quadratic twist by a determinant-method estimate.


However the theorem is not a black-box match.


First mismatch: Petit's setup assumes
  F(x,z)=x^3+A x z^2+B z^3
is irreducible over Z, equivalently the base curve has trivial rational 2-torsion in the relevant line-locus argument. Stage37 has
  F(x,z)=x^3-x z^2=x(x-z)(x+z),
with full rational 2-torsion.


In Petit's proof irreducibility is used to identify rational points on the line locus of the determinant-method surface with the excluded diagonal. For the congruent-number curve extra line components must be re-audited; the theorem cannot simply be quoted.


Second mismatch: Petit's almost-minimal regime requires the small-point parameter alpha<1/120. Stage37 currently has only the coarse physical adapter hhat(P)=O(log B) and no theorem placing both A,C uniformly in Petit's near-minimal d^{1/8+alpha} window.


RESULT_229AC_PETIT_BLACKBOX_APPLICABILITY=NO.


8. Even an idealized Petit transfer misses the Stage37 exponent


Petit's off-diagonal determinant-method term has the exponent
  X^{13/32+45 alpha/4+epsilon}.                  (229AC.12)


Ignore, counterfactually, both mismatches of Section7 and grant the same exponent directly to the relevant physical independent-pair support.


At alpha tending to 0, the best power is
  gamma_Petit=13/32.


With X~B^2 this becomes
  B^{13/16+o(1)},                                (229AC.13)
still far above B^{1/3}.


More generally, if an independent argument first improved the twist envelope to
  d<=B^beta,
the idealized Petit exponent would meet the one-third target only if
  beta(13/32+45 alpha/4)<1/3.                    (229AC.14)


In the optimistic alpha->0 limit this requires
  beta<32/39 approximately 0.8205128.            (229AC.15)


Current beta=2 is far outside this range.


RESULT_229AC_PETIT_IDEALIZED_CURRENT_SCALE=NO_GO.
RESULT_229AC_PETIT_OPTIMISTIC_TWIST_CUTOFF_THRESHOLD=beta<32/39.


For comparison, retained229Q's one-small-point Le Boudec route needs the even stronger optimistic cutoff beta<2/3. Neither cutoff is currently available.
