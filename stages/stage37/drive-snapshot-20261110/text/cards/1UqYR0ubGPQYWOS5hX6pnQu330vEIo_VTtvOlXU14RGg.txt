2. Uniform short-vector lemma for a norm-zero projective root line
Fix a positive odd integer t all of whose prime divisors are 1 modulo 4. For each root rho modulo t of rho^2=-1 mod t, define
  Lambda(t,rho)={(r,s) in Z^2 : r=rho*s (mod t)}.
For every nonzero vector v=(r,s) in this lattice,
  r^2+s^2 = (rho^2+1)s^2 (mod t) =0 (mod t),
so its positive integer squared Euclidean length is at least t.
Consequently DISTINCT lattice points in Lambda(t,rho) have mutual Euclidean separation >=sqrt(t).
A disjoint-disc packing argument gives, for every real Q>=0,
  #{(r,s) in Lambda(t,rho): r^2+s^2<=Q}
   <= (2*sqrt(Q/t)+1)^2
   << 1+Q/t.                                                   (229BE.1)
This bound is UNIFORM in t and rho. The point is that the norm-congruence itself forces the shortest nonzero vector to have length >=sqrt(t); no generic lattice-shape assumption or independently charged CRT density is used.
The number of roots rho^2=-1 mod t is 2^{omega(t)} when t is odd and all p|t split, and zero otherwise. For primitive Euclid parameters gcd(r,s)=1, t|r^2+s^2 forces gcd(s,t)=1; hence r/s mod t exists and selects precisely one such rho. Therefore, after summing projective roots,
  #{primitive (r,s), opposite parity, r>s>0,
     r^2+s^2<=Q, t|r^2+s^2}
   << 2^{omega(t)}(1+Q/t)                               (229BE.2)
for odd t (zero if a forbidden prime occurs).
Proof does not rely on an unspecified external counting theorem.


3. Fixed outer triple: an exact shared-t divisor switch with no moving-scale loss

  q=r^2+s^2, {X,Y}={2rs,r^2-s^2}.

  q<= (B/H)*t.                                             (229BE.3)
