  X0: X^2+Y^2=Z^2,

  -K_Xtilde0 = pi^* O_X0(1).
It also proved that on the primitive positive physical chart [X:Y:Z:T:S]=[x:y:z:e:R], the standard projective sup-height is exactly R.


The current task is not to extend the 229J identity chain. It is to rematch a concrete post-229J theorem input against the exact non-split Q-toric form and to certify the generic degree of the simultaneous selector cover.


2. Santens 2026 now applies to the non-split reversed base with the physical height


Tim Santens, “Manin's conjecture for integral points on toric varieties,” Proc. London Math. Soc. 133 (2026), e70203, first published 26 Aug 2026, DOI 10.1112/plms.70203, proves bounded-height asymptotics for smooth proper toric varieties over number fields without assuming that the relevant Neron-Severi torus is split. The paper explicitly notes that the universal-torsor method is used with nonsplit T_NS and that D=empty recovers a proof of Manin's conjecture for toric varieties.


Theorem 1.1 allows a height corresponding to a big divisor. On Xtilde0 take
  L = pi^* O_X0(1) = -K_Xtilde0.
This is big. Pull back the standard adelic sup metric from O_X0(1). On the positive physical dense-torus points this height is exactly
  H_L = max(x,y,z,e,R) = R,
by 229F/229J.


Therefore the previous 229J caveat “Batyrev–Tschinkel theorem species applies but an exact leading constant for the physical sup metric was not claimed” is no longer a height-applicability obstacle for this Santens theorem species: the physical sup metric itself is an admissible adelic height for the big line bundle L.

3. Fixed finite local test functions are available


Santens's height-zeta setup permits a finite set S of places at which the local factor f_v is prescribed; at finite places f_v is locally constant, and outside S it is the indicator of the chosen integral model. Thus fixed finite collections of p-adic residue neighbourhoods on the non-split toric base can be inserted into the same height-zeta framework.


Consequently, after 229J, the obstacle on the reversed X0 base is no longer “can a non-split toric asymptotic see fixed local congruence conditions at all?”. It can, for each fixed finite local support.

4. The two selector squareclasses are geometrically independent; generic cover degree is exactly 4


229J gave on the split torus, after the degree-two isogeny p=uv, q=v/u, the squareclasses
  F_plus  = (1+p^2)(1+q^2),
  F_minus = (p^2-1)(q^2-1).


Work over Qbar(p,q). Consider the prime divisor p-i. Then
  ord_{p-i}(F_plus)=1,
  ord_{p-i}(F_minus)=0.
Hence F_plus is not a square and [F_plus] is nonzero in Qbar(p,q)^*/Qbar(p,q)^{*2}.


Consider the prime divisor p-1. Then
  ord_{p-1}(F_minus)=1,
  ord_{p-1}(F_plus)=0.
Hence F_minus is not a square.


If [F_plus]=[F_minus], then F_plus*F_minus would be a square; but at p-i its valuation is 1. Thus [F_plus] and [F_minus] are linearly independent over F_2.


Therefore
  [Qbar(p,q)(sqrt(F_plus),sqrt(F_minus)) : Qbar(p,q)] = 4.
The simultaneous double cover is generically degree 4 and geometrically integral on the dense torus. Since any squareclass dependence on the original Q-torus would persist after base change and pullback to the split torus, the original simultaneous selector cover is also generically degree 4.

The missing theorem IS one of:

a theorem for the actual special four-quadric physical surface in seven projective coordinates, exploiting its special structure and exact R-height strongly enough to beat the existing B^(1/2+epsilon) endpoint bound / Stage37 threshold.


Do not start another identity-only continuation from the degree-4 certification. The next legal geometry move is a theorem with quantitative growing-support or exact-height saving.
