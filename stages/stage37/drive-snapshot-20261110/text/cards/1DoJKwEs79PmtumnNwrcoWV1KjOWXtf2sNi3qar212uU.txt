2. Bonolis–Pierce–Woo strongly-genuine specialization theorem


New source checked:
Dante Bonolis, Lillian B. Pierce, Katharine Woo,
“Genuine and strongly genuine polynomials: With an application to the persistence of Galois groups under specialization,” arXiv:2607.01969.


Their strongly n-genuine theorem gives, for an appropriate polynomial F(Y,X_1,...,X_n), an O(B^(n-2)) bound for the (n-1)-tuples of integer partial specializations x' for which the remaining polynomial F(Y,X_1,x') is reducible over the algebraic closure. A finite-field analogue is also proved. The companion n-genuine theorem gives the same B^(n-2) scale for complete splitting over Q after partial specialization.


This is a real fixed-power reducibility theorem and is stronger than generic type-II ambient counts in its own quantifier regime.


However the Stage37 endpoint predicate has a different quantifier. For one exact occupied M3 point, the condition is pointwise:
  X^2 + D^2 = R^2,
equivalently w=D/X lies in the degree-two Pythagorean image.


Pointwise solvability/reducibility of the specialized one-point fibre does not imply that a polynomial with one variable left free becomes identically reducible after a partial specialization. Therefore the O(B^(n-2)) strongly-genuine exceptional-specialization theorem cannot be applied directly to the exact M3 occupied source.


No exact population/height-preserving adapter from the Stage37 source to the BPW partial-specialization exceptional set was found.

3. Exact target-ratio scale envelope


Let w=a/b>0 be in lowest terms and suppose w in T_Pyth. Then there is a unique positive primitive Pythagorean hypotenuse c=c(w) satisfying
  a^2+b^2=c^2.


For every occupied pair (X,D) with D/X=w, coprimality of a,b forces
  (X,D)=k(b,a)
for a positive integer k.


Because w is Pythagorean, this pair is endpoint-active and
  sqrt(X^2+D^2)=k c.


Under the exact physical cutoff R<=B, necessarily
  k c <= B.
Hence the occupied-pair weight satisfies the deterministic exact bound
  n_B(w) <= floor(B/c(w)).                         (229I.1)


Since c(w) is comparable to the standard projective height H(w)=max(|a|,|b|),
  n_B(w) << B/H(w).                                (229I.2)


This uses only the exact ratio definition and physical height; it does not replace the occupied measure by an ambient one.


RESULT_229I_TARGET_RATIO_SCALE_ENVELOPE=PROVED_EXACT.


4. Why the envelope still does not transfer ambient Hilbert sparsity


The scale envelope is too weak to convert the ambient target sparsity into the needed M3-weighted deficit.


Using 229H/Mah,
  #{w in T_Pyth : H(w)<=H} << H (log H)^5.
On a dyadic height shell H~C, (229I.2) gives weight at most B/C per target value, while Mah gives at most O(C(log B)^5) target values. Thus one shell costs at most O(B(log B)^5), and summing O(log B) shells gives only
  sum_{w in T_Pyth} n_B(w) << B (log B)^6.          (229I.3)


For this specific Pythagorean cover, the elementary Euclid parametrization improves the ambient cardinality to O(H), and correspondingly improves (229I.3) only to
  sum_{w in T_Pyth} n_B(w) << B log B.              (229I.4)


Both are far weaker than the retained endpoint ceiling P(B)<<B^(1/2+epsilon), so neither gives any new endpoint exponent, any PAIR fixed-power deficit, or any global counting credit.


Equivalently, define the target occupancy factor
  alpha_B(w)=n_B(w)c(w)/B.
Then 0<=alpha_B(w)<=1 by (229I.1), and
  sum_{w in T_Pyth} n_B(w)
    = B sum_{w in T_Pyth} alpha_B(w)/c(w).
Ambient Hilbert/thin-set results control how many target w occur in the ambient height range; they do not force the realized alpha_B(w) to be small on the exact M3 image.
