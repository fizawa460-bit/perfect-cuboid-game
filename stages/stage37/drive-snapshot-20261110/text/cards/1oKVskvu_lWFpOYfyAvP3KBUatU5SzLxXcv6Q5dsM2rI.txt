2. Fixed-Q cone family: Q does not freeze the tau modulus


For every integer n>=1 define
A=2n^2-1,
Bv=2,
C=2,
d=2n,
D=4n.                                                     (CF.1)


Then A is odd and Bv,C are even, so the retained parity pattern holds.
Moreover
ABv+AC+BvC
=2A+2A+4
=4(2n^2-1)+4
=8n^2
=2d^2,                                                     (CF.2)
hence
D^2=2(ABv+AC+BvC)
and the exact 229BX outer-gap cone is satisfied.


All odd pairwise gcds of A,Bv,C are 1. Therefore
q_UV=q_Uz=q_Vz=1,
Q=1,                                                        (CF.3)
so this is one fixed-Q desaturated-cone family.


Put
s=A+Bv,
t=A+C,
u=Bv+C.
Then exactly
s=t=2n^2+1,
u=4,
g_A=gcd(s,t)=2n^2+1,
g_B=gcd(s,u)=1,
g_C=gcd(t,u)=1.                                             (CF.4)


Also
H_A=A^2+2d^2=(2n^2+1)^2=g_A^2.                              (CF.5)


Hence even with Q fixed identically to 1, the full tau modulus g_A is unbounded and varies with n.

3. Full opposite-gcd factorization


Define
lambda_U := gcd(A,S)=gcd(A,Bv+C),
lambda_V := gcd(Bv,S)=gcd(Bv,A+C),
lambda_z := gcd(C,S)=gcd(C,A+Bv).                            (CF.6)


Because exactly two of A,Bv,C are even, S is odd. Therefore all three lambda_i are odd.


They are pairwise coprime.
For example, if a prime p divided lambda_U and lambda_V, then
p|A, p|Bv, p|S,
hence p|C=S-A-Bv, contradicting gcd(A,Bv,C)=1.
The other pairs are cyclic.


Now let
L_J := gcd(Omega,S)=gcd(ABvC,S).                             (CF.7)


Any prime p|L_J divides S and at least one gap.
It cannot divide two gaps: if p|A and p|Bv, then p|S would force p|C, again contradicting gcd(A,Bv,C)=1.
Thus p divides exactly one gap, and its full p-adic valuation in gcd(Omega,S) is exactly the p-adic valuation of the corresponding lambda_i.
Therefore
L_J=lambda_U lambda_V lambda_z                             (CF.8)
as an equality of full positive integers, not merely radicals.


Since 229CC defined
J=rad_odd(gcd(Omega,S)),
and L_J is odd,
J=rad(lambda_U) rad(lambda_V) rad(lambda_z).                (CF.9)


Moreover the three radicals in (CF.9) are pairwise coprime and are exactly the previously defined matched overlap radicals
r_U=rad(lambda_U),
r_V=rad(lambda_V),
r_z=rad(lambda_z).                                          (CF.10)


RESULT_229CF_EXACT_FULL_J_GCD_FACTORIZATION=PROVED.
This strictly strengthens 229CC's scalar radical identity J=rad_odd(gcd(Omega,S)).


4. Exact -2 root lines modulo the full opposite gcds


Consider lambda_U.
Modulo lambda_U,
A=0,
Bv+C=0.
Using
A(Bv+C)+Bv C=2d^2,
we obtain
Bv C == 2d^2 mod lambda_U,
and C==-Bv, hence
Bv^2+2d^2 == 0 mod lambda_U.                                (CF.11)


Also
gcd(lambda_U,Bv d)=1.
Indeed if p|lambda_U and p|Bv, then p|A,Bv,S and hence p|C, impossible.
If p|lambda_U and p|d, then (CF.11) forces p|Bv, again impossible.


Therefore there is a unique residue
rho_U == Bv*d^(-1) mod lambda_U
for the endpoint, satisfying
rho_U^2 == -2 mod lambda_U,
and
Bv == rho_U d mod lambda_U.                                 (CF.12)


Cyclically, after choosing one of the two gaps opposite the lambda-gap,
there are exact root lines modulo lambda_V and lambda_z as well.


For every prime power p^e||lambda_i, -2 is a nonzero quadratic residue modulo p, hence Hensel lifting gives exactly two roots modulo p^e.
By CRT,
#{rho mod lambda_i : rho^2==-2 mod lambda_i}
=2^omega(lambda_i)
=B^o(1),                                                     (CF.13)
uniformly because lambda_i<S<B.


In particular every odd prime p|J satisfies
(-2/p)=1.                                                     (CF.14)


RESULT_229CF_J_FULL_GCD_MINUS2_ROOT_LINE_ROUTER=PROVED_EXACT.
RESULT_229CF_ALL_J_PRIMES_SPLIT_FOR_MINUS2=PROVED.


5. Fixed-lambda physical packing


Fix lambda_U=m and one root rho^2==-2 mod m.
Every physical endpoint in this fiber gives
Bv == rho d mod m,
with 0<Bv<B and 0<d<B.


The lattice
Lambda(m,rho)={(X,Y) in Z^2 : X==rho Y mod m}
has the property that every nonzero vector satisfies
m | X^2+2Y^2.
Hence its weighted norm is at least sqrt(m), and standard ellipse packing gives
#{(X,Y) in Lambda(m,rho):0<X,Y<B}
<< 1+B^2/m.                                                   (CF.15)


For a fixed pair (Bv,d),
H_B=Bv^2+2d^2=(A+Bv)(Bv+C)                                  (CF.16)
is fixed.
There are at most tau(H_B)=B^o(1) ordered positive factor pairs
(s,u)=(A+Bv,Bv+C).
Each such pair forces
A=s-Bv,
C=u-Bv,
and 229BU reconstruction gives at most one positive physical endpoint.


Summing the 2^omega(m)=B^o(1) roots,
#{P:R<=B, lambda_U(P)=m}
<< B^o(1)(1+B^2/m).                                          (CF.17)


The same estimate holds cyclically for lambda_V and lambda_z.
RESULT_229CF_FIXED_FULL_OPPOSITE_GCD_PHYSICAL_PACKING=PROVED.


6. J-large consequence and quantitative firewall


Since
J=rad(lambda_U)rad(lambda_V)rad(lambda_z)
with pairwise-coprime factors, if J>K then at least one lambda_i satisfies
rad(lambda_i)>K^(1/3),
hence
lambda_i>K^(1/3).                                            (CF.18)


However unrestricted summation of (CF.17) over m>M gives only
B^o(1) sum_{M<m<B}(1+B^2/m)
<< B^(1+o(1)) + B^(2+o(1)) log(B/M),                         (CF.19)
which has no useful one-third saving at the current thresholds.


Thus the new J router is structurally exact but quantitatively insufficient by naive modulus summation.
It is weaker in modulus power than the 229CB tau square-modulus router, so it must not be promoted to a counting result without additional occupied-modulus or selector correlation.


RESULT_229CF_J_ROOT_LINE_PACKING_ALONE_TOO_WEAK=PROVED_QUANTITATIVE_FIREWALL.
