2. Sub-block square-modulus packing lemma


The key point is that 229CG's lattice packing does not require the chosen modulus to equal the full opposite gcd.


Fix a positive odd integer m and consider U-color endpoints satisfying


m|A,
m|u=Bv+C,
gcd(m,2d)=1.                                                  (CQ.8)


Put


Xopp=Bv-C.


The retained exact norm identity is


Xopp^2+8d^2=u(u+4A).                                         (CQ.9)


Therefore


m^2 | Xopp^2+8d^2.                                           (CQ.10)


Since 2d is invertible modulo m^2, define


sigma == Xopp*(2d)^(-1) mod m^2.


Then


sigma^2 == -2 mod m^2,
Xopp == 2sigma d mod m^2.                                    (CQ.11)


For any fixed m, the number of possible roots sigma modulo m^2 occurring on endpoints is at most


2^omega(m)=B^o(1).                                           (CQ.12)


For each root, (Xopp,d) lies in the rank-two lattice


Lambda(m,sigma)={(X,Y): X==2sigma Y mod m^2}


of determinant m^2.


Every nonzero lattice vector satisfies
m^2 | X^2+8Y^2,
so its weighted norm is at least m. The same ellipse/lattice packing argument as 229CG gives


#{(Xopp,d): |Xopp|<B,0<d<B}
 << 1+B^2/m^2.                                               (CQ.13)


For fixed (Xopp,d), put N=Xopp^2+8d^2.
From (CQ.9), u is a positive divisor of N and


A=(N/u-u)/4,
Bv=(u+Xopp)/2,
C=(u-Xopp)/2.                                                 (CQ.14)


There are tau(N)=B^o(1) divisor choices. Remaining integrality, positivity, chamber and physical selectors only delete candidates, and 229BU reconstructs at most one endpoint from a surviving labelled gap triple.


Therefore uniformly for every fixed m satisfying (CQ.8),


#{P:R<=B, m|A(P), m|u(P), gcd(m,2d(P))=1}
 << B^o(1)(1+B^2/m^2).                                      (CQ.15)


Cyclic versions hold for (Bv,t) and (C,s).

This strictly generalizes the use of 229CG's packing mechanism: full-gcd equality m=lambda_i is sufficient but not necessary.
