2. Tau collision adapter — exact host test


Relevant source:
lean/OAI/NumberTheory/EgyptianFractions/CollisionCounting.lean
lean/OAI/NumberTheory/EgyptianFractions/RoughModulusCollision.lean
lean/OAI/NumberTheory/EgyptianFractions/RoughModulusBlocks.lean
lean/OAI/NumberTheory/EgyptianFractions/CompositeBadModuli.lean


The generic collision theorems require one common finite domain A for all moduli/maps under consideration.
For a modulus q, the hypothesis is a high collision energy or equivalently a sufficiently small image of that same common A.
The off-diagonal double count then uses the fact that any fixed distinct pair of elements of A can collide for only restrictedly many moduli.


The block implementation in RoughModulusBlocks is explicit about this structure: all bad moduli in a dyadic block are tested against one fixed divisor list. CompositeBadModuli first transfers the q-dependent badness to that fixed base and only then performs the collision double count.


229CB supplies a different type of datum. For a physical endpoint P with g_A(P)=m it supplies the unary membership
(A(P),d(P)) lies on one of the 2^omega(m) root lines modulo m^2.
Equivalently, for the q-dependent fiber
E_q={P:q=m(P)^2 in the relevant A-router},
the slope/root image is small on E_q.


This does not imply a small image or high collision energy on one common endpoint set E independent of q.
If one defines E_q and uses the root-line pigeonhole inside E_q, the domain itself changes with q, so exceptional_maps_card_le / moduli_rpow_energy_card_le cannot be invoked.
If one instead takes one global E, 229CB guarantees only that the endpoint(s) realizing q lie in the root set; it gives no lower bound saying a positive q-dependent fraction of all E lies in that root set. Hence the required collision-energy inequality is not discharged.


RESULT_229CE_TAU_ROOT_LINE_TO_COMMON_COLLISION_DIRECT_TRANSFER=FAILS_AT_COMMON_DOMAIN_HYPOTHESIS.
This is a host-implication failure, not a theorem that no additional Stage37 structure could ever create the needed common base.


Precise missing theorem:
TAU_FIXED_BASE_COLLISION_AMPLIFICATION=
for each relevant dyadic modulus block, construct one selector-exact finite common pool A_D independent of q and prove that every realized/bad q in that block inherits high residue/projective-slope collision energy on A_D, with an off-diagonal restricted-modulus multiplicity B^o(1), while preserving the physical endpoint-to-q accounting at R<=B.


Without this amplification/nesting step, Problem337 collision machinery cannot count the 229CB occupied tau moduli.


3. J large-gcd adapter — exact host test


Relevant source:

The symmetric weighted_large_gcd_removal decomposes a Jacobi quadratic energy over one common odd-squarefree column set by gcd(n,t)=d and bounds the large-d tail through shorter quotient support.


The repository also contains genuinely bilinear two-column-set machinery: coprime_jacobi_bilinear_moebius, coprimeDivisorJacobiRow, and exists_gcd_separation_scales. Thus the earlier 229CD audit is sharpened: the absence of two different column sets is NOT the obstruction.


The remaining mismatch is sparse matched incidence.


Stage37 has, endpoint by endpoint,
r_U(P)=gcd(u_U(P),w_u(P)),
r_V(P)=gcd(u_V(P),w_t(P)),
r_z(P)=gcd(u_z(P),w_s(P)),
and
J(P)=r_U(P)r_V(P)r_z(P)=rad_odd(gcd(Omega(P),S(P))).


A large J therefore yields a large gcd on at least one matched edge
(u_i(P),w_i(P))
after a three-label pigeonhole.


Across endpoints, however, these matched pairs form a sparse incidence set
E_i subset U_i x W_i.
The Ostmann bilinear forms sum over Cartesian products S x T with factorable coefficient weights a(n)b(t), together with arithmetic coprimality/divisibility conditions. They do not accept an arbitrary incidence indicator 1_{(n,t) in E_i} as a host input.


Replacing the Stage37 matched-edge sum by the full Cartesian U_i x W_i creates cross-endpoint pairs not present in the physical source and does not preserve the desired lower bound/counting direction. Arbitrary decomposition of the incidence matrix into product weights has no proved subpower complexity bound.


RESULT_229CE_J_MATCHED_GCD_TO_OSTMANN_DIRECT_TRANSFER=FAILS_AT_SPARSE_INCIDENCE_FACTORIZATION_HYPOTHESIS.


Precise missing theorem:
J_SPARSE_MATCHED_INCIDENCE_BILINEARIZATION=
either
(a) prove that the selector-conditioned Stage37 matched incidence matrix for one label has B^o(1) factorization/biclique complexity compatible with Ostmann's bilinear gcd/Jacobi bounds,
or
(b) prove a sparse-graph extension of the relevant large-gcd removal estimate with the same quotient-support saving,
or
(c) construct a different injective/bounded-fiber encoding of J-large endpoints into a genuine common Cartesian/Jacobi energy for which cross terms are harmless and the R-height measure is retained.


The existence of bilinear GcdSeparation modules removes the weaker “only symmetric energy exists” concern, but it does not solve the matched-edge host.
