2. General odd-norm CM square-factor theorem


Let
  K=Q(i),
  E_d:Y^2=X^3-d^2X,
  T0=(0,0),
and let
  phi=[alpha], alpha=a+bi in Z[i],
with N(alpha) odd.


The divisor of X on E_d is
  div(X)=2(T0)-2(O).                             (229W.1)


Consider
  F_alpha = (X composed with [alpha])/X in K(E_d)^*.
Then
  div(F_alpha)
   =2D_alpha,
where
  D_alpha=[alpha]^*(T0-O)-(T0-O).               (229W.2)


Under Pic^0(E_d)≅E_d and the principal polarization, pullback by [alpha] acts as the dual endomorphism [bar(alpha)]. Hence
  [D_alpha]=[bar(alpha)]T0-T0.                   (229W.3)


Now [i] fixes T0. Since N(alpha)=a^2+b^2 is odd, a and b have opposite parity, so a-b is odd. Therefore
  [bar(alpha)]T0=[a-bi]T0=(a-b)T0=T0.
Thus
  [D_alpha]=0.


Hence D_alpha is principal over K. Choose h_alpha in K(E_d)^* with
  div(h_alpha)=D_alpha
and normalize h_alpha(O)=1.


Using the standard formal parameter at O, the differential of [alpha] is multiplication by alpha, so
  F_alpha(O)=alpha^(-2).
Therefore
  F_alpha=(h_alpha/alpha)^2.                    (229W.4)


Equivalently, for every nonexceptional Q,
  X([alpha]Q)/X(Q) is a square in K.             (229W.5)


RESULT_229W_ODD_CM_X_RATIO_IS_K_SQUARE=PROVED_EXACT.


This proof is intrinsic and does not require an explicit division polynomial.
