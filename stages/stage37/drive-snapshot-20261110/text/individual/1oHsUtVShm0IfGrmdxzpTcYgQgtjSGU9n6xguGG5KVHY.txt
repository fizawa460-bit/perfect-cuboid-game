2. Exact K=1 face and space triangles


Relabel an exact K=1 endpoint as in M3-T11-176:
h_1=h_2=1, h_3=h,
R=h*tau.


The primitive space triangle complementary to e_3 is
u=e_3/h,
v=f_12/h,
u^2+v^2=tau^2,
gcd(u,v)=1.                                           (181.1)


For the physical face (e_1,e_2,f_12), put
g=gcd(e_1,e_2),
A=e_1/g,
B=e_2/g,
n=f_12/g.


Then
A^2+B^2=n^2,
gcd(A,B)=1,
and the triple (A,B,n) is primitive.                 (181.2)


Because the whole endpoint is primitive, one has
gcd(h,g)=1.                                          (181.3)


Indeed, any prime dividing both h and g divides e_3 through h and divides e_1,e_2 through g, contradicting gcd(e_1,e_2,e_3)=1.


The same physical diagonal satisfies
f_12=g*n=h*v.                                        (181.4)


Using (181.3), h divides n and g divides v. Define
k=n/h=v/g.                                           (181.5)


Therefore exactly
n=h*k,
v=g*k,
gcd(h,g)=1.                                          (181.6)


It follows immediately that
k=gcd(n,v),                                          (181.7)
h=n/gcd(n,v),                                        (181.8)
g=v/gcd(n,v),                                        (181.9)
and
f_12=lcm(n,v).                                       (181.10)


These identities are on the exact physical endpoint measure.

5. Large-k Euclid allocation and saturation firewall


Because the primitive face hypotenuse n is odd and h is odd, k is odd.


From the primitive space triangle,
v^2=(tau-u)(tau+u).                                  (181.25)


For every odd prime power p^a||k, one has p∤tau by (181.18). Therefore p cannot divide both tau-u and tau+u, since their gcd divides 2*tau.


Thus the p^(2a) contribution from k^2|v^2 is allocated wholly to exactly one of tau-u or tau+u.


There are unique pairwise coprime positive integers k_-,k_+ with
k_-*k_+=k,
k_-^2 | tau-u,
k_+^2 | tau+u.                                      (181.26)


Choosing the larger one gives q=max(k_-,k_+), hence
q>=sqrt(k),
q^2>=k.                                               (181.27)


On the residual (181.24),
q^2
>B^(1/6-xi-epsilon_k).                               (181.28)


Equivalently,
u == +tau mod k_-^2
or
u == -tau mod k_+^2.                                 (181.29)


However this is not a new rare square-divisor event. In every primitive Pythagorean triangle the normalized factors tau±u are themselves squares or twice squares in Euclid parameters. The large q in (181.26) is therefore a saturated Euclid-root allocation of the already-forced large shared divisor k.


No lattice-density, square-divisor-density or root-line probability may be charged from (181.26)-(181.29) without a genuinely new same-endpoint correlation theorem.
