2. Two support sets and the incidence graph


Let E_sc(B;C) be the exact primitive canonical endpoints in the current small-c, retained-large-R branch.


Define the occupied pair support
P_rt(B;C)
:=
{(r,tau): some endpoint in E_sc(B;C) has canonical max Euclid root r and common residual tau}.


Define the active labeled content-tau support
A_ht(B;C)
:=
{(h1,h2,h3,tau): some endpoint in E_sc(B;C) has these labeled contents and tau}.


Join p=(r,tau) in P_rt to a=(h1,h2,h3,tau) in A_ht if at least one exact endpoint realizes both labels.


This is a same-endpoint incidence graph. No ambient pair or divisor tuples are inserted.


3. Degree on the fixed-content side


Retained M3-T11-017 proves that for each fixed labeled content triple (h1,h2,h3) and fixed tau, the number of exact endpoint candidates is B^o(1), because the three primitive Pythagorean hypotenuses
D1=tau*h2*h3,
D2=tau*h1*h3,
D3=tau*h1*h2
are fixed and each has only divisor-many oriented primitive Pythagorean directions.


Every adjacent canonical r must be realized by one of those exact endpoints. Therefore uniformly,


deg_A(h1,h2,h3,tau) <= B^o(1).                       (169.1)


Consequently,


|P_rt(B;C)| <= B^o(1) |A_ht(B;C)|.                  (169.2)


Thus passing from active fixed-content/tau packets to occupied (r,tau) pairs never creates a support saving by itself.


4. Degree on the fixed-(r,tau) side and the exact residual moving content


M3-T11-168 proves the same-measure fixed-pair endpoint bound


m_(r,tau)(B)
<=
(1+r/tau)(1+r/sqrt(tau)) B^o(1).                    (168.12)


Every distinct active labeled content packet adjacent to (r,tau) contains at least one such endpoint. Hence


deg_P(r,tau)
<=
(1+r/tau)(1+r/sqrt(tau)) B^o(1).                    (169.3)


On the current branch r<tau, this becomes


deg_P(r,tau)
<=
(1+r/sqrt(tau)) B^o(1).                              (169.4)


The proof of168 identifies exactly where the polynomial factor comes from:


- after fixing selected face/sign/parity and (r,tau), the companion Euclid root s has only B^o(1) possibilities because s^2==-r^2 mod tau and r<tau;
- then the selected content c_i is fixed up to B^o(1);
- its coprime factorization c_i=h_j*h_k has only B^o(1) choices;
- the only remaining polynomially long content coordinate is h_i, with
  h_i <= 2*sqrt(2)*r/sqrt(tau);
- after h_i is fixed, D_j,D_k are fixed and the remaining oriented primitive directions cost only B^o(1).


Therefore the factor r/sqrt(tau) is not an anonymous loss. It is the length of one explicit residual moving-content coordinate h_i, up to absolute/subpower factors.


Using r^2/tau<=2C,


deg_P(r,tau) <= C^(1/2) B^o(1),                     (169.5)


and hence


|A_ht(B;C)| <= C^(1/2) B^o(1) |P_rt(B;C)|.          (169.6)


Together, (169.2) and (169.6) give the exact power-scale comparison


P_rt  <= A_ht * B^o,
A_ht   <= C^(1/2) * P_rt * B^o.


The two supports differ by at most the single residual h_i dimension.


5. Weighted occupied-pair reduction


Summing the fixed-pair bound (168.12) over the actually occupied pair support gives the exact same-measure upper bound


#E_sc(B;C)
<=
B^o(1) *
sum_{(r,tau) in P_rt(B;C)}
(1+r/tau)(1+r/sqrt(tau)).                            (169.7)


On the live branch r<tau,


#E_sc(B;C)
<=
B^o(1) *
sum_{(r,tau) in P_rt(B;C)}
(1+r/sqrt(tau)).                                     (169.8)


Define the weighted occupied support


W_rt(B;C)
:=
sum_{(r,tau) in P_rt(B;C)}
(1+r/sqrt(tau)).


Then a sufficient same-measure closure theorem for branch(B) is simply


W_rt(B;C)=o(B^(1/3)).                                (169.9)


This is the correct pair-support target. Replacing W_rt by the crude bound C^(1/2)|P_rt| is legal but loses the information that small c_max pairs carry smaller fiber weight.


At the standard C=B^(1/6+xi), the crude raw-cardinality sufficient condition is


|P_rt(B;C)|
=
o(B^(1/4-xi/2)).                                     (169.10)


No such raw support bound is currently proved.


6. Why M3-T11-015 through 019 do not automatically remove the weight


M3-T11-015 works after fixing a labeled content triple and its small Gaussian coefficient packet. It writes
D_i=tau*c_i,
A_i=B_i Z_i,
N(B_i)=c_i,
N(Z_i)=tau,
then decomposes the equal-norm tau parts Z_i into the four relative-orientation cores G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3. The triple-spin and six-bilinear selectors of 015-019 act on this fixed-content/coefficient packet.


For the fixed-(r,tau) fiber above, after c_i=h_j*h_k is fixed, the residual h_i changes
c_j=h_i*h_k,
c_k=h_i*h_j.


Thus varying h_i changes the norms and orientations of the two small coefficient factors B_j and B_k. It is not motion purely inside the equal-tau cores G_0,...,G_3.


Therefore the fixed-content triple-spin selector cannot be summed over the h_i interval for free. A new theorem would be required that is uniform in this one moving content/coefficient parameter.


This is exactly the kind of same-measure two-edge correlation that M3-T11-006 left open: one physical content is seen simultaneously in the two neighboring directions, and the two appearances may not be multiplied as independent savings.


Accordingly:

This is a transfer firewall, not a rejection of 015-019. Those cards remain valid and remain a direct route on the fixed-content side.

<=
C^2 B^(1/12-delta0+o(1)).                           (169.12)


At the standard scale its exponent is

So routing 015-019 through the raw (r,tau) support loses exactly the residual C^(1/2) moving-content dimension and is strictly weaker than using the active-tau theorem directly.


This proves the route decision:

- use the (r,tau) route only if a genuinely new theorem controls W_rt or the residual h_i motion;
- do not claim that the near-B size of r^2*tau plus the old triple-spin selector automatically yields pair-support density.
