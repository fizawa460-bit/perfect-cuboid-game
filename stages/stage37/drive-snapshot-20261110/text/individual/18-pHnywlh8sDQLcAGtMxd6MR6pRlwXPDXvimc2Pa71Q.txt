2. New moment obstruction
For every fixed s>0,
M_s(B)>=k kappa^s B^(1/4+s/2).                  (172.1)
This follows by summing only the156 carrier edges.
Consequently a square-root-budget moment theorem
M_s(B)<=B^(1/2+o(1))                            (172.2)
is impossible for every fixed s>1/2. At s=1/2 the lower and upper exponents coincide; no existence of such an upper theorem is asserted.


For the useful threshold C=B^gamma, gamma=1/6+xi, 0<xi<1/12, Markov and (172.2), if available, would give
E_{c>C}(B)<=B^(1/2-s gamma+o(1)).                (172.3)
A fixed-power improvement below the retained M3 lower benchmark B^(1/3) requires
s gamma>1/6, equivalently s>1/(1+6xi)>2/3.       (172.4)
But (172.2) requires s<=1/2 by (172.1). These are incompatible.
Even taking the largest non-refuted order s=1/2 gives certificate exponent
1/2-gamma/2 >3/8 >1/3.                         (172.5)
Thus the entire untruncated c^s moment method with the unstratified square-root budget cannot close branch(A), throughout the useful threshold interval. This rejects a method, not the single-threshold tail conjecture. It is stronger than merely prohibiting the all-C uniform 1/C tail.


For a different proposed upper exponent alpha_s, the necessary carrier constraint is alpha_s>=1/4+s/2. Markov needs alpha_s-s gamma<1/3. A necessary condition for such a certificate is therefore
s(1/2-gamma)<1/12.                              (172.6)
This is only necessary, not sufficient. Improved low-order moments with alpha_s<1/2, or endpoint/residual-masked moments, are not ruled out. The positive-power margin absorbs B^o(1), as corrected in171.


3. Threshold-capped receiver and its limits
Define on the same raw measure
W_s(B;C)=sum_{e in E(B)} min(c(e),C)^s.
Exactly
W_s(B;C)=s integral_0^C u^(s-1) E_{c>u}(B) du,
and E_{c>C}(B)<=C^(-s) W_s(B;C).                (172.7)
The integral is a finite-sum layer-cake identity; the strict inequality at a single boundary has no effect on the integral.
At C=B^gamma<kappa sqrt(B),156 now forces only
W_s(B;C)>=k B^(1/4+s gamma).                    (172.8)
A conjectural W_s<=B^(1/2+o(1)) is not contradicted by this carrier when s gamma<=1/4; Markov would close when s gamma>1/6.
The compatibility window is
1/(6 gamma)<s<=1/(4 gamma).                     (172.9)
It is nonempty. For example s=5/(24 gamma) has carrier lower exponent11/24, and a hypothetical square-root upper budget would give tail exponent7/24<1/3.
No capped upper theorem is proved. The elementary bound W_s<=C^s|E(B)| only returns E_tail<=B^(1/2+o(1)); truncation itself creates no saving. Other raw families can impose further restrictions. An endpoint-specific mask also avoids (172.1) only after a genuine measure adapter; the raw carrier is not known to survive that mask.
A capped moment bound is a distribution theorem including the low-c population, and can be stronger than a desired one-threshold tail. Treat it as an optional certificate, not an equivalent reformulation of the tail and not a new bound.
