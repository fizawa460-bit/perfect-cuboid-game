2. Complete moving-family direction count


Fix ell>=1 and suppose the other primitive leg is x>=ell.


If ell is odd, the primitive triple has x even and p odd. Then
(p-x)(p+x)=ell^2.
Both factors are positive odd integers. Every triple with this fixed ell determines a factor pair u*v=ell^2, u<v, via
u=p-x, v=p+x,
and conversely the factor pair determines
p=(u+v)/2, x=(v-u)/2.
Primitivity and x>=ell can only remove factor pairs. Hence the number of unordered primitive directions with short leg ell is at most tau(ell^2).


If ell is even, it is the even leg of the primitive triple, while x and p are odd. Put
u=(p-x)/2, v=(p+x)/2.
Then u,v are positive integers and
u*v=ell^2/4.
Again a factor pair determines (p,x), so the number is at most tau(ell^2/4)<=tau(ell^2).


Allowing both leg orders costs at most a factor 2. Therefore, uniformly for 1<=E<=B,
A_axis(B,E) <= 2 sum_{ell<=E} tau(ell^2).


For every eta>0 the elementary divisor bound tau(n)<<_eta n^(eta/2) gives tau(ell^2)<<_eta ell^eta after renaming eta. Consequently
A_axis(B,E) <<_eta E^(1+eta) <= E B^eta.
Equivalently,
A_axis(B,E) = E B^o(1)
uniformly for E<=B.
No fixed-leg constant, moving direction family, content, lift or physical parent multiplicity is hidden in this arithmetic family sum.
