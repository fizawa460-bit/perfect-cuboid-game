## 1. Uniform nonzero factors, no fresh hypothesis
From138, A_i=e_i+f_i are all odd and primitive. Set F_0=sum A_i^2, F_i=F_0-2A_i^2.
Odd squares are1 mod8. Thus
 F_0=3 mod8, F_i=1 mod8 for i=1,2,3.
All four factors are nonzero, and C_A=-product F_j=5 mod8 is nonzero. This holds for all primitive physical signed witnesses, all contents and all charts; no finite-u/fresh theorem is needed.139's square-divisor enumeration is therefore finite throughout the physical population.


## 2. Positive physical directions have S and all v_i nonzero
Use d_i=2x_i y_i, S=sum d_i, v_i=S-2d_i, sum x_i^2=1, x_i^2+y_i^2=1. Positive three edges ensure each x_i>0 and each y_i!=0, hence each d_i!=0.
The134 actual signed-sphere identity gives
 T(v)=(SE+P)^2-S^2 P(4S-P)=0,
 E=sum_(i<j)v_i v_j, P=product v_i.
If S=0, then T=P^2 forces P=0, but v_i=-2d_i are all nonzero: contradiction.
If v_i=0, then P=0 and T=(SE)^2. Since S!=0, E=0. Writing the other roots v_j,v_k gives E=v_j v_k=0. Hence a second root vanishes. The remaining root equals S, giving d_k=(S-v_k)/2=0 for its index, again a contradiction.
Nonzero S on the old nondegenerate branch was already retained in ROUTE41; do not credit it as a new counting theorem. This direct physical proof explains why positivity is essential and permits the next elementary integer exclusion without a new u chart assumption.


## 3. No single Heron factor absorbs the whole squared physical diagonal
Directly, F_0/R^2=3+S and F_i/R^2=1+v_i.
Cauchy gives |S|<=2sqrt(2), so
 0<3-2sqrt(2)<=F_0/R^2<=3+2sqrt(2)<6.
If R^2|F_0, its integer quotient is between0 and6 and is3 mod8, because R is odd and R^2=1 mod8. Thus the quotient is3, forcing S=0, impossible.


Each A_i^2 lies in(0,2R^2], so -2<F_i/R^2<4 (the weaker closed bounds also suffice). If R^2|F_i, its integer quotient in[-2,4] is1 mod8, hence1. This forces v_i=0, impossible.
Therefore
 R^2 does NOT divide F_j for any j=0,1,2,3,
although139 proves R^2 divides their product.
This is an exact same-R set exclusion, not a character probability or a support power bound. It applies to every primitive positive endpoint and each signed face witness. Boundary points with a zero edge can violate full-factor exclusion; they are excluded from this theorem.


Equivalently each gcd(R^2,|F_j|) is a proper divisor of R^2. Do not infer a fixed power gap from properness: the missing prime-power can be bounded while R grows. No uniform R^epsilon split has been proved.
