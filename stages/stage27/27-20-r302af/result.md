
```text
D_d = sum_lambda mu_d(lambda)
```

on the slice. Decompose it exactly as

```text
mu_d(lambda) = D_d/m + nu_d(lambda),
sum_lambda nu_d(lambda)=0.
```

For every pair `(u,v)`, the uniform term contributes

```text
(D_d/m) * sum_{lambda mod m}
  e_m(-lambda (u^2-v^2))
 = D_d * 1_{u^2=v^2 (mod m)}.
```

```text

 = sum_{r,s: rs!=0 mod m}
     C_d(r,s) * nu_hat_d(r s)
```

with the exact admissibility/common-parent/2-primary data retained, where

```text
nu_hat_d(h)=sum_lambda nu_d(lambda)e_m(-lambda h).
```

This is the canonical dispersion form of the remaining branch. It prevents the uniform main term from being mistaken for a new saving and shows precisely what a same-measure theorem must suppress: the inverse-`a'` **pushforward discrepancy**, tested only against the sheared coefficient class generated by the actual Fourier vector.
