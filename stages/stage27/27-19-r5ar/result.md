The repaired r5aq theorem gives, for one fixed admissible modulus \(k\) and one fixed coefficient cell,

\[
T(X;k)\ll_\varepsilon (Xk)^\varepsilon
\left(\frac Xk+\sqrt X\right).
\]

In the Stage19 application, \(X\le B\) and the exact r5ah scale relation

\[
R=(h/\varepsilon)\kappa L
\]

with \(h,L\ge1\) and \(\varepsilon\le2\) gives

\[
\boxed{k=\kappa\le2B}.
\]

Hence, after renaming \(\varepsilon\),

\[
T(X;k)\ll_\varepsilon B^\varepsilon
\left(\frac Xk+\sqrt X\right).
\]

This route tests whether summing this physically weighted estimate over a dyadic squarefree-kernel block already gives a fixed-power saving.

## 1. Naive dyadic modulus sum

For \(K\le k<2K\), summing the Stage19 fixed-modulus theorem gives

\[
\sum_{k\sim K}T(X;k)
\ll_\varepsilon B^\varepsilon
\left(
\sum_{k\sim K}\frac Xk
+K\sqrt X
\right).
\]

Since \(\sum_{k\sim K}1/k\ll1\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\sim K}T(X;k)
\ll_\varepsilon B^\varepsilon
\left(X+K\sqrt X\right).
}
\]

Equivalently, before Stage19 specialization one may retain the harmless dyadic prefactor \((XK)^\varepsilon\).

The first term has lost the fixed-\(k\) \(1/k\) gain: the \(K\) possible moduli exactly consume it at dyadic scale. The second boundary term is no better.

The unrestricted four-variable hyperbolic population itself is only \(X^{1+o(1)}\), so after taking the minimum with the ambient count the dyadic result still gives no fixed-power density saving.

Thus physical weighting alone does not produce a growing-\(K\) density gain after the modulus is allowed to vary.

## 2. Why the union bound is throwing away arithmetic information

For actual Stage19 survivors, \(\kappa\) is not an arbitrary modulus chosen from \([K,2K)\). It is generated by S1:

\[
a c_s^2\sigma^2+b c_n^2\nu^2
=\kappa c_0^2c'^2.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\kappa=\operatorname{sf}
\left(a c_s^2\sigma^2+b c_n^2\nu^2\right).
}
\]

So once \((a,b,c_s,c_n,\nu,\sigma)\) is fixed, the modulus is unique. The dyadic union bound discards exactly this self-generated squareclass relation and pays for \(K\) unrelated candidate moduli that do not coexist for a fixed outer cell.

This is the next arithmetic interface.

## 3. Consequence for the half-power attack

The next theorem must avoid paying one full factor of the number of candidate moduli. Two concrete routes remain:

1. count outer cells for which the self-generated kernel
   \[
   \operatorname{sf}(a c_s^2\sigma^2+b c_n^2\nu^2)
   \]
   lies in a dyadic range, while retaining the exact product budget; or
2. prove an averaged squareclass/residue estimate in which the \(1/\kappa\) local density survives summation because \(\kappa\) is tied to S1 rather than independently selected.

No fixed-power outer-support theorem is proved here.
