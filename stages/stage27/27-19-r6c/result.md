## Theorem

Let `(e,x,y)` be a primitive Stage19 object with exactly two integral faces and integral space diagonal `R`.  Write the two successful face diagonals as `D_x,D_y`, so

\[
D_x^2+y^2=R^2,\qquad D_y^2+x^2=R^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{R\text{ is odd}}
\]

and every prime divisor of `R` satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\mid R\implies p\equiv1\pmod4.}
\]

Equivalently, primitive occupied space diagonals lie in the multiplicative semigroup generated by primes `1 mod 4`.

## Proof: the prime 2

If `2|R`, then `R^2` is divisible by four.  From

\[
D_x^2+y^2\equiv0\pmod4
\]

and the square residues modulo four, both `D_x` and `y` must be even.  The second norm equation similarly forces both `D_y` and `x` even.  Since

\[
e^2=D_x^2-x^2,
\]

`e` is also even.  Thus `gcd(e,x,y)>=2`, contradicting primitivity.

## Proof: inert odd primes

Let `p=3 mod 4` divide `R`.  Since

\[
D_x^2+y^2\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

and `-1` is not a quadratic residue modulo `p`, one must have

\[
p\mid D_x,\qquad p\mid y.
\]

The second norm equation gives

\[
p\mid D_y,\qquad p\mid x.
\]

Then `e^2=D_x^2-x^2` is divisible by `p^2`, hence `p|e`.  Again `p` divides all three physical edges, contradicting primitivity.

Therefore no prime `2` or `3 mod 4` divides `R`.

## Strength and limitation

This is a genuinely new support-level consequence of primitivity.  It is not the Stage15 squareclass predicate in different coordinates.

However it is not a fixed-power sparsity theorem.  By the classical prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions, the support host already contains

\[
\pi(B;4,1)\sim \frac{B}{2\log B}
\]

prime values `R<=B`.  Hence the condition alone has size `B^{1-o(1)}` and cannot imply

\[
S_2(B)\ll B^{1-\delta}
\]

for any fixed `delta>0`, much less beat the already-certified `B^{1/2+o(1)}` whole-family upper bound.

The prime-support restriction may be retained as a filter in later arguments, but its logarithmic sparsity must not be multiplied into the Stage14 half-power bound unless a same-measure theorem explicitly transfers it through the existing host.
